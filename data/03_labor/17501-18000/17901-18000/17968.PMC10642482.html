Metastases in the Pineal Region: A Systematic Review of Clinical Features, Management Strategies, and Survival Outcomes

Abstract

BACKGROUND:

Pineal region metastases are rare but often cause severe neurologic deficits. Surgical resection and chemoradiotherapy can provide therapeutic benefit. We investigated the literature to analyze clinical characteristics, management strategies, and survival of adult patients with pineal region metastases.

METHODS:

PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and Cochrane were searched following the PRISMA guidelines, including studies reporting clinical outcomes of patients with pineal region metastases. Clinical presentation, management, and survival were reviewed.

RESULTS:

We included 31 studies comprising 47 patients. Lung cancer (29.8%) and carcinomas of unknown origin (14.9%) were the most frequent primary tumors. In 48.9% of patients, symptomatic pineal metastases preceded primary tumor diagnosis. Headache (67.4%) and confusion (46.5%) were the most common symptoms. Parinaud syndrome (46.5%) and hydrocephalus (87.2%) were noted. Biopsy (65.9%) was preferred over resection (34.1%), and shunting strategies used were endoscopic third ventriculostomy (43.9%) and ventriculoperitoneal (26.8%). Eleven patients (32.3%) received adjuvant chemotherapy and 32 (68%) received radiotherapy. Posttreatment improvement in symptoms (56.6%) and hydrocephalus (80.5%) were noted. In patients who received adjuvant chemotherapy/radiotherapy, significant improvement in posttreatment performance status occurred with both biopsy (P < 0.001) and resection (P = 0.007). No survival differences were reported between surgery and biopsy (P = 0.912) or between complete and partial resection (P = 0.220). Overall survival was neither influenced by surgical approach (P = 0.157) nor by shunting strategy (P = 0.822). Mean follow-up was 8 months and median overall survival 3 months. Only 2 cases (4.8%) of pineal metastasis showed recurrence.

CONCLUSIONS:

Pineal region metastases carry significant morbidity. Biopsy or surgical resection, combined with adjuvant chemotherapy/radiotherapy and/or shunting, may significantly improve performance status.

INTRODUCTION

Tumors in the pineal region are rare, accounting for ≤1% of all intracranial neoplasms in adults.1–3 The pineal region represents an unusual site for metastases, carrying an estimated prevalence of 0.4%–3.8, with the predominant origination from lung malignancies.4,5 Pineal lesions often present with hydrocephalus, from the obliteration of the cerebral aqueduct, and visual disturbances, from dorsal midbrain compression. The radiologic findings can be nonspecific; thus, histopathologic confirmation is often required to differentiate metastatic from primary tumors and guide treatment.6,7

Surgery plays a diagnostic, therapeutic, and palliative role in the management of brain metastases, generally in combination with chemotherapy, radiotherapy, or other systemic treatments.8–10 In pineal region metastases, the goal of surgery includes obtaining a diagnosis, relieving ventricular obstruction, and/or maximizing tumor resection.6,11,12 The complex anatomy of the pineal region poses a challenge, but advances in imaging, microsurgical techniques, and surgical approaches aim to reduce surgical risks and maximize tumor resection in eligible patients.11–15

Available data on pineal region metastasis are predominately derived from single-institution reports of limited patients and are inadequate to provide neurosurgeons and neuro-oncologists with practical guidelines. In this systematic review, we searched the literature to review clinical features and management strategies of pineal region metastases and to assess their influence on clinical improvement and survival.

METHODS

Literature Search

A systematic review was performed following the 2020 PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines.16 PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and Cochrane were searched from inception to April 3, 2021 using the combination of the Boolean operators “OR” and “AND” and the search terms: “pineal” and “metastases.” Collected articles were exported to Mendeley; duplicates were removed.

Study Selection

A priori inclusion and exclusion criteria were specified. Studies were included if they 1) presented ≥1 patients aged ≥18 years with histologically confirmed secondary metastases involving the pineal region, as explicitly mentioned within their text, or extracted from radiologic images; 2) reported data on clinical features, treatment strategies, and survival outcomes; and 3) were written in English. Studies were excluded if they were 1) reviews, autopsy reports, or animal studies or 2) studies with insufficient data on management and outcomes.

Two reviewers (C.O. and P.P.) independently screened titles and abstracts of all extracted citations and assessed full texts of articles satisfying the inclusion criteria. Disagreements were settled by a third reviewer (A.S.H.). Eligible articles were included on the prespecified criteria. References were checked to retrieve additional relevant studies.

Data Extraction

One reviewer (C.O.) extracted data from included studies, which were then verified independently by 2 additional reviewers (P.P. and A.S.H.). Patient-level data included author, year, study design, age, gender, primary tumor, time interval between primary tumor diagnosis and pineal metastasis, presenting symptoms, Parinaud syndrome, hydrocephalus, imaging, lesion size, extent of surgery and approach, cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) diversion, chemotherapy/radiotherapy protocols, clinical (reduction of symptoms and hydrocephalus) and radiologic treatment responses, pretreatment and posttreatment Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG-PS), pineal metastases recurrence and leptomeningeal disease (LMD) onset, progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and survival status.17,18 Extent of surgery was described as follows: complete resection for 100% tumor removal and partial resection for <100% tumor removal. Treatment responses were evaluated at 6 months or at last available follow-up. Radiologic responses were assessed in patients undergoing biopsy with adjuvant chemotherapy/radiotherapy on the proposed RANO (Response Assessment in Neuro-Oncology) criteria for brain metastases, describing changes in lesion volume as follows: complete response (complete resolution), partial response (decreased volume), stable disease (no volume change), and progression disease (increased volume).18

Data Synthesis and Quality Assessment

The primary outcomes of interest were management strategies, clinical improvement, and survival of patients with pineal region metastases. The level of evidence of each article was evaluated following the 2011 Oxford Centre for Evidence-Based Medicine guidelines.19 Meta-analysis was precluded because all included studies had levels IV–V of evidence, and hazard ratios could not be deducted. For each article, risk of bias was independently assessed by 2 reviewers (P.P. and O.B.A.) using the Joanna Briggs Institute checklists for case reports and case series.20,21

Statistical Analysis

The software SPSS version 25 (IBM Corp., Armonk, New York, USA) was used for all analyses. Continuous variables are summarized as medians or means and ranges, and categorical variables as frequencies and percentages. A 2-sample weighted t test was performed to assess differences between pretreatment and posttreatment ECOG-PS scores in patients undergoing surgical resection or biopsy with adjuvant chemotherapy and/or radiotherapy, and between posttreatment ECOG-PS scores based on surgery versus biopsy, complete versus partial resection, and chemoradiotherapy versus chemotherapy alone. χ2 tests were used to appraise differences in rates of CSF diversion, symptom improvement and ventriculomegaly relief, and LMD onset between surgical resection versus biopsy only. Time intervals between surgery and pineal metastases recurrence (PFS curve) or death (OS curve) were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method, and survival analyses conducted with the log-rank test. A 2-tailed P value <0.05 was considered significant for all analyses.

RESULTS

Study Selection

Figure 1 shows the flow diagram of the literature search and study selection. The search yielded 2256 citations (PubMed, 374; EMBASE, 977; Scopus, 886; Cochrane, 19), and 31 articles were finally included. Six were case series (including 22 patients) and 25 were case reports, categorized as level IV and V evidence, respectively (Table 1).5,22–51 Quality assessment returned low risk of bias for all included studies (Supplementary Table 1).

Demographics and Clinical Characteristics

A total of 47 patients with pineal region metastases were analyzed (Table 2). Patients were predominantly men (55.3%), with a median age of 57 years (range, 27–82 years) and unimodal age distribution. Metastases mostly originated from lung cancer (29.8%), and primary neuroectodermal tumors (PNETs, 10.6%), whereas 7 patients (14.9%) were diagnosed with carcinomas of unknown primary (CUP). In 48.9% of patients (23/47), the detection of pineal metastases preceded the diagnosis of primary tumors. The most common presenting symptoms were headache (67.4%), altered mental status/confusion (46.5%), and vision disturbance/diplopia (27.9%). Parinaud syndrome was diagnosed in 20 patients (46.5%) and hydrocephalus in 41 (87.2%) at clinical presentation, and 17 patients (36.1%) were diagnosed with both. Among 19 patients with reported lesion volumes, median maximal tumor diameter was 2.1 cm (range, 1.2–3.6 cm). On imaging, pineal region metastases mostly showed T1 contrast-enhancement heterogeneity (76.6%) more than homogeneity (6.7%), and/or computed tomography (CT) calcifications (16.6%). Concurrent brain parenchyma metastases were reported in 6 patients (12.8%), single in each case, and involving the cerebellum (n = 3, 6.4%), the cerebellopontine angle (n=1, 2.1%), the frontal lobe (n = 1, 2.1%), and the brainstem (n = 1, 2.1%).27,33,38,44,48,50

Management Strategies

Table 3 summarizes management strategies and treatment outcomes. Although most patients (31/46, 65.9%) underwent biopsy alone for histopathologic diagnosis, surgical resection was pursued in 16 patients (34.1%). Of those who underwent resection, complete resection was achieved in 13 patients (81.3%), whereas 3 had a partial resection (18.7%). Surgical approaches were described in 14 patients, comprising supracerebellar infratentorial (50%), frontal translateral ventricular (14.3%), frontal transcallosal (14.3%), occipital transtentorial (14.3%), and subtemporal (7.1%) approaches. Among the 41 patients with hydrocephalus, CSF diversion was achieved with endoscopic third ventriculostomy (ETV) in 18 patients (43.9%), ventriculoperitoneal shunt (VPS) in 11 (26.8%), and temporary external ventricular drainage (EVD) in 3 (7.3%). Rates of CSF diversion were not significantly different (P = 0.257) between patients receiving biopsy (83.9%) and patients undergoing surgical resection (62.5%). Most patients (28/32, 87.5%) underwent CSF diversion and surgical biopsy/resection within the same surgical session. Three patients (9.4%) received CSF diversion (2 VPS and 1 ETV) before surgical biopsy/resection and 1 patient (3.1%) underwent EVD after surgical resection because of persistence of hydrocephalus but died within 1 month. In the other 2 patients receiving temporary EVD concomitant with surgical resection of pineal lesions, the shunt was permanently removed within 5 days after resolution of the underlying ventriculomegaly. Nine patients (21.9%) with ventriculomegaly did not undergo CSF diversion. A total of 35 patients (74.5%) received the adjuvant therapies of chemotherapy (11/34, 32.3%) and/or radiotherapy (32/47, 68%). Chemotherapy protocols were reported in only 5 patients (45.5%): 2 (18.2%) received carboplatin and pemetrexed for lung cancer,35,49 2 received vincristine, adriamycin, andcyclophosphamide (18.2%) for lung cancer30 and PNET,42 and 1 received 5-fluorouracil (9.1%) for colorectal cancer.27 Based on radiotherapy protocols, 20 patients (42.5%) underwent whole-brain radiotherapy, including the 6 patients with oligometastatic brain disease, and 12 (25.5%) underwent stereotactic radiosurgery.

Treatment Outcomes

Median ECOG-PS scores were 2 before treatment and 1 after treatment.17 Differences in weighted means of pretreatment and posttreatment ECOG-PS showed significant improvement in posttreatment scores both for patients undergoing surgical resection with adjuvant chemotherapy/radiotherapy (P = 0.007) and for patients receiving biopsy with adjuvant chemotherapy/radiotherapy (P < 0.001). No differences in posttreatment ECOG-PS scores were found in patients with or without concurrent parenchymal brain metastases (P = 0.395), in patients undergoing surgery versus biopsy (P = 0.912), in patients undergoing complete versus partial resection (P = 0.072), and in patients receiving chemotherapy and radiotherapy compared with patients receiving chemotherapy only (P = 0.585). Seventeen patients (17/30, 56.6%) achieved posttreatment symptom improvement after surgical resection and relief of tumor mass effect (4/17, 23.5%), after CSF diversion and relief of hydrocephalus in patients undergoing biopsy (2/17, 11.8%), or after both surgical resection and CSF diversion (11/17, 64.7%). No differences in symptomatic improvement were noted between surgical resection versus biopsy (P = 0.075). In addition, 29 patients (29/36, 80.5%) achieved radiologic improvement of ventriculomegaly after CSF diversion, with no statistical differences between surgery versus biopsy (P = 0.197). Among the 28 patients with available follow-up radiologic response assessment, complete response occurred in 7 (25%), partial response in 10 (35.7%), stable disease in 3 (10.7%), and progression disease in 8 (28.6%).18

Ten patients (22.2%) developed LMD after surgery, with no significant differences (P = 0.293) based on biopsy (8/10, 80%) versus resection (2/10, 20%). Of the 13 patients who underwent complete resection, 2 (15.4%) experienced pineal metastasis recurrence. In this review, the mean follow-up time was 8 months (range, 1–124 months), with both median PFS and OS of 3 months (range, 1–124 months). The OS was 41% at 3 months, 31% at 6 months, and 24% at 12 months. In patients receiving adjuvant chemotherapy/radiotherapy, neither biopsy versus surgery (P = 0.912), nor extent of resection (P = 0.220) showed significant survival differences (Figure 2). In addition, survival was not statistically correlated with concurrent presence of parenchymal brain metastases (P = 0.512), the chosen surgical approach (P = 0.157), or with the CSF diversion modality (P = 0.822). A total of 29 patients (61.7%) had died at last follow-up.

DISCUSSION

Pineal region metastases are rare entities, historically reported only in autopsy series and with scarcely available clinical data.52–54 In this review, we found that both surgical resection and biopsy, with adjuvant chemotherapy/radiotherapy and CSF diversion, significantly improve symptoms and performance status, with no statistical differences in survival. No survival benefits were also described among the different CSF diversion protocols, all feasible and safe options in these patients with hydrocephalus.

Metastatic spread to the pineal region has been theorized to be favored by the local absence of the blood-brain barrier and/or by the specific organotrophic seeding of cancer cells.55,56 Among the 18 different primary tumors included in this review, lung cancer, both small-cell (7/14, 50%) and non-small-cell (7/14, 50%), was the most common (29.8%); this finding likely reflects its overall prevalence in patients with brain metastases.57 In contrast, we found low rates of primary breast cancer and melanoma, with higher frequencies of other tumors less commonly metastasizing to the brain (e.g., esophageal and gastric). Our findings further corroborate that some neoplasms may show specific organotrophic seeding to the pineal region compared with the rest of the central nervous system (CNS).58 The rarity of pineal region metastases challenges the identification of selected tumor histomolecular microenvironments predisposing to such uncommon metastatic locations.59 Similarly, little is known regarding potential mediators inducing and sustaining dormant disseminated tumor cells in the CNS and, specifically, in the pineal region.60 A better understanding of these mechanisms and the growth factors involved may support the development of innovative therapies targeting circulating tumor cells and/or metastatic niches, aimed at improving the prevention and treatment of all CNS metastases. Seven cases of CUP (14.9%) were also reported, and symptomatic pineal metastases preceded the diagnosis of the primary tumors in 48.9% of patients, likely because of the symptomatic nature of presentation, with a higher rate of Parinaud syndrome and hydrocephalus at the time of diagnosis.

In both pediatric and adult populations, pineal region masses manifest clinically because of compression of adjacent structures.61 In this review, the median metastatic volume was 2.1 cm, which led to debilitating neurologic symptoms in almost all patients.12,13 Grozinsky-Glasberg et al.32 reported the only case of a clinically silent pineal metastasis from lung PNET, incidentally detected on brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) at follow-up, measuring 1 cm and not compressing the surrounding structures. This finding further confirms the correlation between lesion size and patient clinical status. In line with previous reports on pediatric papillary tumors, we found high rates of hydrocephalus (87.2%) from cerebral aqueduct obstruction and Parinaud syndrome (46.5%) from medial longitudinal fasciculus compression. These clinical results mirror the complex pineal region anatomy.62

Pineal region metastases often show central necrotic cores and peripheral contrast-enhancing rims on MRI (76.6% in our cohort), and/or are calcified on CT (17.2% in our cohort), similar to other brain metastases.4,63,64 Imaging features of pineal metastases are nonspecific, usually comparable to high-grade gliomas and pinealoblastomas on MRI scans, and with ordinary nonsignificant pineal calcifications in elderly patients on CT scans.65,66 Hence, in patients with undefined pineal masses, a full metastatic workup should be performed.

In this review, most patients underwent only biopsy (65.9%), contrary to previous large series of mixed pineal region tumors reporting higher rates of surgical resection.12,13 These differences may be caused by the poor surgical eligibility of patients with pineal metastases, often presenting with known systemic diseases and/or severe comorbidities. We further noted that complete resection (27.6%) was achieved more frequently than partial resection (6.4%), perhaps because of the well-defined margins and the limited infiltrative nature of lesions observed in candidates for surgical debulking. In these cases, accurate surgical planning is essential, aimed at achieving optimal tumor exposure and control of neighboring vascular structures. In addition, similarly to other neuro-oncology lesions, intraoperative extemporaneous histopathologic examination may guide the surgical workflow and extent of surgical resection.67,68 However, these data were lacking among our included studies, advocating the need for a better evaluation of the role of intraoperative histologic analysis for pineal region metastases, which may lead to different surgical aggressiveness based on primary tumor diagnosis. As we found in our cohort, the supracerebellar infratentorial route is the preferred surgical corridor for pineal lesions, because it effectively provides direct access to the pineal region and third ventricle, offering safe arachnoid dissection from the vein of Galen and other deep vein tributaries while approaching the tumor.13,69,70 We found no correlation between survival and surgical approaches (P = 0.157); our analysis is likely limited by our small sample size and needs to be validated with larger studies.

One of the most urgent treatment goals in patients with obstructive hydrocephalus from pineal masses is to restore the normal CSF flow.69,71 In this review, similar to large surgical series of heterogeneous pineal tumors, ETV was preferred (43.9%), because it decreases the risks of infection, overshunting, and peritoneal metastatic seeding compared with VPS.13,66 In addition, endoscopic ETV further allows hydrocephalus relief and lesion biopsy/resection in a single surgical approach.72,73 Transient EVD was emergently performed in only 3 patients (7.3%) presenting with severe symptoms and poor prognoses; whereas one died soon thereafter,39 the other 2 improved and remained hydrocephalus free without requiring further shunting.40,42 CSF diversion was not pursued in 9 patients with ventriculomegaly (21.9%); whereas 2 patients rapidly deteriorated and died,22,46 the remaining 7 showed spontaneous relief of the aqueduct obstruction after tumor resection.5,24,28,30,41 Our findings also showed significant improvement in posttreatment ECOG-PS after biopsy (P < 0.001) and surgical resection (P = 0.007), coupled with substantial decrease in symptoms (56.6%) and hydrocephalus (80.5%). We ascribe these positive outcomes, comparable between both surgical strategies, mainly to the prompt management of hydrocephalus, which affected most of our pooled patients (87.2%). In addition, the lack of survival benefit among the different CSF diversion modalities (P = 0.822) strengthens our hypothesis: the topmost priority is normalizing the intracranial pressure regardless of the modality adopted. Hence, the surgical management of pineal metastases can first aim to treat hydrocephalus, weighing the benefits of endoscopy to the surgeon’s expertise.74,75

Most of our pooled patients received adjuvant therapies (74.5%). Although stereotactic radiosurgery (25.5%) was exclusively performed after biopsy in patients not eligible for resection, adjuvant whole-brain radiotherapy (42.5%) followed both biopsy and surgical removal with the purpose of local tumor control.5,43,44 Despite the fact that chemotherapy may simultaneously treat primary tumors and brain metastases and also reach the pineal region because of the local blood-brain barrier absence, only a few of our pooled patients received adjuvant chemotherapy (32.3%). We presume that most institutions prioritized local treatments (surgery and radiotherapy) directed against symptomatic pineal lesions, which frequently preceded primary tumor diagnoses.29,48,50 Overall, adjuvant treatments favored tumor shrinking (60.7%) in patients undergoing biopsy, and the few reported cases of tumor progression (28.6%) were likely a result of the aggressive nature of primary histologic diagnoses.5,22,29,34,42,46,49 In addition, in patients receiving adjuvant chemotherapy/radiotherapy, we noted no survival benefits of surgical resection over biopsy (P = 0.220), further suggesting the superior role of adjuvant therapies in the management of metastatic diseases compared with the extent of surgery. Surgical debulking remains an option in pineal region metastases for relieving symptoms, but biopsy with adjuvant treatments should be preferred for minimizing undesired surgical risks while tailoring systemic therapies.

Although pineal metastatic recurrence rarely occurred in our cohort (4.4%), 61.7% of patients died because of metastatic disease progression, with a median OS of 3 months. We also noted lower 12-month survival (21%) compared with previous large series of brain metastases (39%–46%).76,77 Our findings were probably limited by the small sample size and the short follow-up times across included studies, because most patients marked as “alive” were followed-up for only 1–3 months after tissue diagnosis. The sporadic cases of long-term survival were mostly observed in patients with less aggressive primary tumors receiving optimal systemic treatment strategies.5,26,29,44,47 Our results emphasize the dismal prognoses of pineal region metastases, thus advocating for less invasive treatment strategies primarily intended to ease symptoms and improve baseline functional status.

We found a 22.2% rate of LMD in our cohort, in line with previous series reporting higher incidence of leptomeningeal dissemination in patients with primary pineal region tumors.78–81 The close proximity of the pineal gland to the third ventricle and the quadrigeminal cistern has been accounted for the leptomeningeal spread of cancer cells through CSF pathways.5,78 CSF diversion may increase the risk of distal seeding, especially in VPS, but no direct correlation has been reported.5,80 The benefits of CSF shunting outweigh the potential complications, but the risk of LMD should be considered when treating pineal region metastases with biopsy and resection.

Limitations

Our study has some limitations, primarily because of the paucity of pineal region metastases in the literature. All studies were retrospective case reports and case series, thus subject to selection bias. These studies covered a 52-year period characterized by important advances in neurosurgical and oncologic treatments, which likely introduced confounding variables into our analysis. The small sample size and the heterogeneity of outcome data challenged the statistical power of our study. Our survival analyses were likely prone to potential biasing because of the short follow-up times across included studies. Because of the frequently missing data, we could not appraise the percentages of pineal gland metastases nor analyze the roles of CSF and/or serum studies/biomarkers, rigid versus flexible endoscopic techniques, immune/targeted therapies, the relation between CSF diversion modalities and LMD onset, and the best approaches for preventing and/or treating leptomeningeal dissemination.

CONCLUSIONS

Pineal region metastases rarely occur in oncologic patients but can severely worsen clinical status and survival. Both surgical resection and biopsy, combined with adjuvant therapy, have been shown to increase overall performance status. Benefits of surgical debulking include prompt reduction of mass effect. Both biopsy and surgical resection provide tissue diagnosis and help guide adjuvant chemotherapy/radiotherapy protocols. CSF diversion improves symptoms and performance status and should be considered for all patients with pineal region metastases if clinically indicated. The risk of leptomeningeal dissemination needs to also be considered and managed appropriately. Future studies are encouraged to establish tailored multidisciplinary management strategies based on histomolecular characteristics of pineal region metastases.
