Endoscopic Revision of Gastrojejunal Anastomosis for the Treatment of Dumping Syndrome in Patients with Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

Abstract

Background:

Dumping syndrome is a known complication of Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass (RYGB). Recently, endoscopic gastrojejunal anastomosis (GJA) revision has been employed as a treatment option. The primary aim of this study was to perform a systematic review and meta-analysis for the role of endoscopic GJA revision in patients with RYGB for the treatment of dumping syndrome.

Methods:

Search strategies were developed for PubMed, EMBASE, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library databases from inception through December 2020 in accordance with PRISMA and MOOSE guidelines. Pooled proportions with rates estimated using random effects models were used. Outcomes included pooled technical success, clinical success, adverse events, and rate of reintervention. Heterogeneity was assessed with I2 statistics and publication bias by funnel plot using Egger and Begg tests.

Results:

Six studies (n= 263 patients; 60.25% female) were included (1 prospective and 5 retrospective). Mean age was 46.27±2.54 years. Average patient weight was 95.59±4.78 kg, BMI of 41.43±3.07 kg/m2 and pre-procedure GJA size of 32.23±8.68 mm. Pooled technical and clinical success was 98.15% and 89.5%. Among studies reporting Sigstad scores, endoscopic GJA revision resulted in a significant improvement [mean Sigstad score difference of −9.96 (95% CI, - 19.951 to −0.975); P<0.03]. Mean procedure time was 37.12±10.40 minutes with an intra-procedural adverse event rate of 2.42%. Over a mean follow-up of 8.03±6.87 months, post-procedure adverse events occurred in 2.96% of patients with a reintervention rate of 11.54%.

Conclusion:

This systematic review and meta-analysis suggests endoscopic GJA revision appears an effective and safe treatment for dumping syndrome.

Introduction:

Dumping syndrome is a well-established complication of Roux-en-Y Gastric Bypass (RYGB), occurring in up to 25–50% of patients (1, 2) and can significantly impair health-related quality of life (3). The syndrome is typically characterized by episodes of postprandial hypoglycemia, manifesting with gastrointestinal and vasomotor symptoms of diarrhea, flushing, abdominal pain, tachycardia, fatigue, syncope and seizures (1, 4). Dumping syndrome can be further stratified into ‘early’ and ‘late. Early dumping syndrome typically occurs within 1 hour post-prandially, thought to be related to rapid fluid shifts as a result of rapid small bowel exposure to hyperosmolar contents emptying from the gastric pouch. Late dumping syndrome typically occurs 1–3 hours post-prandially and is suspected to be due to a delayed incretin response (2, 5). Symptoms are variable both in character and severity and traditionally assessed using the Sigstad score (6). The Sigstad scoring system evaluates 16 symptoms scored based on severity and strength of correlation to dumping syndrome (i.e., shock provides the maximum increase in score with +5 points, whereas vomiting is uncommon in dumping syndrome and provides-4 points) (Table 1). The total score ranges between-5 and 34, with a total score of ≥7 suggestive of dumping syndrome and <4 suggestive of alternative etiologies.

Following an extensive evaluation for competing causes of symptoms such as postoperative adhesions or strictures, the methodologic approach to dumping syndrome involves lifestyle alterations, where dietary adjustments toward consuming frequent, low-volume meals while minimizing rapidly-absorbable carbohydrates may be effective (7, 8). When symptoms persist, pharmacologic agents including acarbose and octreotide may help ameliorate symptoms by slowing the release of simple sugars from ingested carbohydrates and reducing risk for post-prandial hyperinsulinemia (7).

A variety of surgical approaches have been pursued to address refractory dumping syndrome, however, methods are varying and include techniques such as gastric bypass reversal, gastric pouch restriction, pancreatic resection, duodenal switch and duodenojejunal bypass (1, 9, 10), however, efficacy and safety have remained variable. Alternatively, endoscopic therapy targeting diameter reduction of a dilated and incompetent gastrojejunal anastomosis (GJA) to delay gastric pouch emptying has evolved as a safe and effective method at addressing weight loss (11, 12). Later, leveraging the mechanism of delayed gastric emptying, endoscopic GJA revision has also been demonstrated as a safe and effective modality for treating symptoms associated with dumping syndrome (13). The primary aim of this study was to perform a structured systematic review and meta-analysis to evaluate the efficacy safety and efficacy of endoscopic GJA revision in patients with RYGB for the treatment of dumping syndrome.

Methods:

Literature Search

This study was prospectively submitted in PROSPERO, an international database of prospectively registered systematic reviews in health and social care. The Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) statement outline reporting guidelines for reporting systematic reviews and meta-analyses was used to report findings (14). Individualized literature search strategies were performed in effort to identify full-text manuscripts and abstracts that evaluated the safety and efficacy of GJA revision for the treatment of dumping syndrome. Individualized systematic searches of PubMed, EMBASE, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library databases were performed from available literature from nception through December 31, 2020. The following search terms included: dumping syndrome OR gastrojejunal anastomosis (GJA) revision OR Transoral Outlet reduction.

Relevant English language full-text articles and abstracts were included, regardless of year of publication, in this systematic review and meta-analysis. Duplicate articles were extracted following the initial literature search, and then the titles and abstracts of all potentially relevant studies were screened for eligibility. Subsequently, the reference lists of studies of interest were manually reviewed for additional articles by cross checking bibliographies. Three reviewers (ANB, RDD and TRM) independently screened the titles and abstracts of all the articles according to predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria. When studies were discovered with incomplete information, contact was attempted with the principal authors to obtain additional data.

Study Selection Criteria

Full-text manuscripts and abstracts were included in this analysis. Only human studies investigating the use of GJA revision for management of dumping syndrome were included. Studies were excluded if determined to have insufficient independent data, including editorials, correspondence letters and reviews. Additionally, case series and reported studies with fewer than 5 patients were excluded to minimize selection bias. To preserve independence of observations, multiple published works from similar authors were evaluated for overlapping enrollment times.

Outcome Measures

The primary outcome measurement in this study was the efficacy and safety of GJA revision for the treatment of dumping syndrome. Efficacy and safety of GJA revision for dumping syndrome were measured by pooled technical and clinical success rates (defined by symptom improvement), difference in pre-and post-procedural Sigstad score assessments and adverse event and reintervention rates. Secondary reported characteristics included baseline patient and procedure characteristics including GJA diameter and duration of procedure (minutes), when available.

Statistical Analysis

This systematic review and meta-analysis was performed by calculating pooled proportions. After appropriate studies were identified through systematic review, the individual study proportion was transformed into a quantity using the Freeman–Tukey variant of the arcsine square root transformed proportion. Then the pooled proportion was calculated as the back transformation of the weighted mean of the transformed proportions, DerSimonian-Laird weights for the random effects model (15, 16). The pooled rates were estimated using random effects models and presented as point estimates (rates) with 95% confidence intervals (17–19). In contrast to fixed effect models, which are used to estimate a common effect, random effect models estimate an average effect, and the variability of the effects represented by their average may have clinical implications.

Additional sensitivity analyses were performed for all measured outcomes including only full text, published manuscripts (abstracts excluded). Further analyses were performed for specific devices used to perform endoscopic GJA revision. All calculated P values were 2-sided, and P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. Tabular and graphical analyses were performed using Comprehensive Meta-Analysis software, version 3 (BioStat, Englewood, NJ). Combined weighted proportions were determined by use of the Stata 15.0 software package (Stata Corp LP, College Station, TX).

Risk of Bias and Quality Assessment

Risk of bias and quality of observational studies was evaluated using the Newcastle-Ottawa Quality Assessment Scale (20). In this study, high quality was defined as a Newcastle-Ottawa Quality Assessment Scale score of ≥4. Three authors (ANB, RDD and TRM) independently extracted data and assessed the risk of bias and study quality for each of the articles. Any disagreements were resolved by discussion and consensus between the three authors.

Investigations of Heterogeneity

Heterogeneity was assessed for the individual meta-analyses using the chi squared test and the I2 statistic (18). Significant heterogeneity was defined as P<0.05 using the Cochran Q test or I2 >50%, with values >50% indicating substantial heterogeneity. Further quantification of heterogeneity was categorized based upon I2 with values of 25%, 50%, and 75% indicating low, moderate, and high amounts of heterogeneity, respectively.

Publication Bias

A funnel plot was created and visually inspected for asymmetry and quantitatively using Egger regression testing to assess for publication bias (21,22). If evidence of publication bias, then the trim and fill method was used to correct for funnel plot asymmetry and provide an adjusted effect (23). The classic fail-safe test was also applied to assess risk of bias across studies.

Results:

Baseline Study Characteristics

A total of 6 studies (n= 263 patients) were included in this meta-analysis (13, 24–28). A PRISMA flow chart of search results is shown in (Figure 1). A total of 4 included studies were full-text published manuscripts with the remaining studies being published abstracts from annual scientific meetings. No randomized controlled trials were included with one prospective study, and the remaining studies being retrospective in nature. Studies from 3 different countries were included with most studies published from academic centers within the United States. Four studies performed endoscopic revision of the GJA using the Apollo OverStitch suturing device (Apollo Endosurgery, Austin, TX, USA). The two remaining studies performed the procedure with the USGI EndoSurgical Operative System (EOS) [USGI Medical, San Clemente, CA, USA] and the StomaphyX device (Endogastric Solutions, Redmond, WA). A complete summary of baseline study and patient characteristics assessing endoscopic revision of the GJA for the treatment of dumping syndrome is highlighted in Table 2.

Patient and Procedure Characteristics

Of the 3 studies that reported gender, the majority (60.25%) of patients were female. Mean age of included patients was 46.27 ±2.54 years with an average weight of 95.59 ±4.78 kg (210.74 ±10.54 lbs). The mean BMI of included patients was 41.43 ±3.07 kg/m2. In the included studies, patients were followed over a mean duration of 8.03 ±6.87 months. Patients, on average, developed symptoms of dumping and underwent endoscopic revision treatment 91.30 ±19.10 months (or 7.61 years) after Roux-en-Y gastric bypass. On endoscopy, the mean size of the gastrojejunal anastomosis prior to treatment was 32.23 ±8.68 mm.

Feasibility, Safety, and Effectiveness of Endoscopic Revision

The pooled technical and clinical success of the endoscopic revision procedure was 98.15% [(95% CI, 94.39 to 99.40); I2=0.00] and 89.50% [(95% CI, 79.17 to 95.03); I2=53.3], respectively (Table 2, Supplemental Figure 1). Mean procedure time for endoscopic revision was 37.12 ±10.40 minutes. The mean size of the GJA decreased significantly post-procedure from 32.23 ±8.68 mm to 8.80 ±1.72 mm; P<0.001. The intra-operative adverse event rate was 2.42% [(95% CI, 0.78 to 7.28); I2=0.00] and post-procedure adverse event rate was 2.96% [(95% CI, 0.77 to 10.64); I2=24.78] (Supplemental Figure 2).

Clinical success was defined by change in Sigstad score in two studies, a 0–4 visual analog scale in 1 study (27), and a quick assessment food tolerance after bariatric surgery questionnaire in 1 study (26). The two included abstracts defined clinical success with unspecified symptom surveys(24, 25) (Supplemental Figure 1). Two studies reported pre- and post-procedure Sigstad scores with a significant improvement in dumping syndrome noted after endoscopic GJA [weighted mean Sigstad score difference of −9.96 (95% CI, −19.95 to −0.975); I2=97.75; P=0.03). This resulted a 74.65% decrease in symptoms of dumping syndrome based upon the validated Sigstad score. Recurrent symptoms requiring reintervention were required in 11.54% [(95% CI, 2.60 to 38.67); I2=84.19] (Supplemental Figure 2). Of the patients requiring reintervention, 70.59% percent of cases involved subsequent endoscopic therapy with the remaining patients requiring surgical revision.

Sensitivity Analyses

Sensitivity analyses were also performed for included data from published studies only. These results were not different from outcomes reported from all studies based upon overlapping CIs (Table 3). Next, USGI and StomaphyX devices were excluded and the performance of the Apollo OverStitch device was evaluated independently. Again, these results are summarized in Table 3 and were not statistically different from cumulative data based upon overlapping CIs.

Quality and Risk of Bias Assessment

Quality assessment for each study was determined to be of high quality (Newcastle-Ottawa Quality Assessment Scale scores ≥4) and demonstrated in Table 2. Publication bias was also assessed by visual inspection of the funnel plot and Egger regression with no evidence of publication bias (Figure 2).

Discussion:

The results of the present systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrate that endoscopic GJA revision is both a technically feasible and effective approach at treating patients with dumping syndrome following RYGB. The pooled technical success and clinical success were 98.15% (94.39 – 99.4%) and 89.5% (79.17 to 95.03%), respectively. Furthermore, the endoscopic approach appears safe and overall adverse events were uncommon (3.1% occurred intraoperatively and 3.8% occurred postoperatively). This is a notable benefit of endoscopy, where efficacy and safety of surgical revision methods for treatment of intractable dumping syndrome remain variable (7, 9, 10, 29). Subsequently, endoscopic treatment of the GJA to address symptoms of refractory dumping syndrome should be considered a valid alternative to surgery.

Endoscopic revision of the GJA initially evolved with a primary intention to reduce rapid gastric emptying as a result of a dilated and incompetent GJA, therefore inducing weight loss (11, 12). Recognizing that rapid gastric emptying is also the primary mechanism contributing to symptoms of early and late dumping syndrome (5), a significant reduction in Sigstad scores following endoscopic GJA revision is unsurprising as improved tissue competence and a reduced GJA diameter result in delayed release of contents from the gastric pouch. Between two studies reporting Sigstad scores, post-revision scores were significantly reduced compared to baseline, with a pooled mean 9.96-point reduction (p=0.03), correlating to a 74.65% improvement. Although medical therapy with either acarbose or octreotide have been shown to induce a similar reduction in Sigstad scoring (86.4% and 58.3%, respectively) (30, 31), it is notable that the included studies reporting Sigstad scores for endoscopic revision reported higher pre-intervention Sigstad scores (13.9–17) (13, 28) compared to medical therapy (2.36–12) (30, 31), suggesting endoscopic patients were more symptomatic prior to intervention.

Endoscopic GJA revision can be achieved using different devices with varying mechanisms, as demonstrated in the included study. The majority of studies included within this meta-analysis evaluated suturing using the Apollo OverStitch device, varying between interrupted (24, 27) and purse-string (13, 28) patterns. Although the purse-string technique has previously been associated with superior weight loss compared to an interrupted pattern (32), differences in efficacy at reducing dumping syndromes remains unknown, particularly as the two studies that reported Sigstad scoring utilized the purse-string pattern. However, additional devices utilizing a plication technique have been studied with success, using either StomaphyX (26) or USGI (25). Among these devices, StomaphX is no longer available whereas USGI remains available. Due to the current paucity of studies available, direct comparisons of efficacy and safety between devices remains lacking and remains a need for future studies.

A few limitations exist for the present study. Despite evaluation with I2, inherent heterogeneity bias remains a consideration for all systematic reviews and meta-analyses and may affect study bias, patient population differences and inter-operator variability in procedure outcomes. Second, characterization of dumping syndrome remains variable and definitions differ between studies, with not all studies reporting standardized scoring such as the Sigstad assessment. Third, the follow-up period was variable between studies, ranging from 1 to 22 months. Longer duration follow-up is needed. Fourth, all included studies were observational in nature and data quality would be improved with the addition of randomized controlled trials. Despite these limitations, the present study has the notable strengths of being the first meta-analysis analyzing pooled data for endoscopic treatment of post-bypass dumping syndrome. Further study may reduce the need for surgical revision in medically refractory dumping syndrome patients as the endoscopic approach becomes more standardized and widely available.

In conclusion, endoscopic GJA revision appears technically feasible, safe and effective at reducing symptoms associated with dumping syndrome in RYGB patients and should be considered as an alternative to more invasive surgical approaches.
