The authors regret that the published version of this article contained errors in Fig. 3, Appendix 19, mmc1 and mmc2 as corrigendum. We provided the corrected Fig. 3, Appendix 19 and descriptions below and reconfirmed all information of mmc1 and mmc2. We highlighted the corrected median sampling year in the new table (Supplementary Corrigendum Table). So the trend of transmitted drug resistance (NRTI, NNRTI, PI) in different regions of China changed accordingly as showed below. The mistakes do not affect the temporal trend of drug resistance prevalence in ART-naive individuals with HIV, and the corrections do not change the interpretation, and conclusions of the paper.

Appendix 19

Temporal trend of drug resistance prevalence in ART-naive individuals with HIV by drug class since 2001.

The section of “3.2 Temporal trend of HIV-1 drug resistance prevalence in ART-naïve individuals in China” is corrected to

China has an overall rising trend of HIV-1 drug resistance in ART-naïve individuals from 2001 to 2017 (Fig. 3). A slow decrease of TDR from 6.43% to 4.00% was observed during 2001-2010, followed by a rapid increase from 4.05% in 2011 to 5.00% in 2017. The trend of resistance to NNRTI decreased from 4.52% to 2.14% during 2001 to 2011 and increased from 2.15% to 3.81% during 2012 to 2017; whereas the trend of resistance to PI was inclined from 0.71% in 2001 to 1.67% in 2017 with a peak (2.14%) in 2011. Moreover, the trends of resistance to NRTI was declined from 3.57% in 2001 to 1.67% in 2017. The trend of NNRTI resistance was well consistent with the overall trend of HIV-1 drug resistance, indicating that HIV-1 drug resistance was mainly driven by NNRTI resistance. The prevalence of three classes of drug resistance in ART-naïve individuals appeared to be more serious and fluctuant in Central China than in South and North China (Fig. 3 and Appendix 19). In Central China, TDR showed a rising prevalence trend from 2001 to 2015. The dynamics showed a rapid decrease from 9.50% in 2001 to 5.48% in 2009, followed by a steep increase from 5.48% in 2009 to 12.86% in 2015 at an average growth of 1.1% per year. The prevalence trends of NNRTI resistances were similar to the overall pattern that had a first decrease and then an increase. A difference was that the prevalence of PI resistance was relatively stable from 2005 to 2015 with a small peak (2.86%) occurring in 2011. In South China, HIV-1 drug resistance showed a rising trend from 2.38% in 2003 to 4.05% in 2017 (Fig. 3). In North China, the overall prevalence of HIV-1 drug resistance has been declining from 6.00% in 2006 to 3.33% in 2017 accompanied by a downward trend in PI resistance from 4.04% to 0.48%.
