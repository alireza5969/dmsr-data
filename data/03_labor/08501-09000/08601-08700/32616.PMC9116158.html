Abstract

Cardiac disease is the primary cause of death in sickle cell disease (SCD). Cardiac abnormalities begin in childhood and progress throughout life. Right and left ventricular (RV, LV) myocardial strain are early markers of systolic dysfunction but are not well investigated among individuals with SCD. The objectives of this review were to 1) identify all published studiesthat have evaluatedventricular myocardial strain, 2) summarize their values, and 3) compare findings with those obtained from controls.From search results of four electronic databases—Medline, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science—42 potential articles were identified, and 17 met eligibility criteria for inclusion. Theevaluated studies demonstrate thatRV and LV myocardial strain are generally abnormal in individuals with SCD compared with controls, despite having normal ejection/shortening fraction.Myocardial strain has been inconsistently evaluated in this population and should be considered any time an echocardiogram is performed.

INTRODUCTION

Sickle cell disease (SCD) is an inherited hemoglobinopathy that affects approximately 100,000 individuals in the United States and an estimated 300,000 newborns worldwide annually.1–3In SCD, recurrent episodes of vascular occlusion by sickled red blood cells,with subsequent inflammation and ischemia,lead to multisystem acute and chronic disease manifestations, including cardiac disease.4–7

Cardiac abnormalities in SCD begin in childhood and progress throughout life, culminating in significant morbidity and premature mortality in adulthood.5, 6, 8–10Many studies have identified cardiac disease as the primary cause of death in SCD.8, 9, 11, 12Despite advances in monitoring and treatment of patients with SCD, there are currently no recommended screening guidelines to assess cardiac function in childhood or adulthood.13 Furthermore, no interventions exist that specifically address early cardiac disease manifestations.14

The extent to which SCD affects the heart and the mechanism of cardiac disease progression are not well understood but represent an area of ongoing research.15–17Cardiac disease manifestations in SCD include structural and functional complications of both the right and left heart.Structural changes such as left ventricular (LV) hypertrophy and left atrial dilationmay begin early in life and can be readily identified by echocardiography in childhood.16, 18–20Diastolic and systolic dysfunctionin SCD are also identified by echocardiography and can be observed throughout the lifespan.Diastolic dysfunction appears earlier in life, while systolic dysfunction usually appears later and is often associated with other co-morbidities, such as systemic hypertension, pulmonary hypertension, and renal dysfunction.15, 16, 21–23

Myocardial strain

Myocardial strain is an echocardiographic measure of cardiac disease. Myocardial strain can measure systolic function of both the right and left ventricles by evaluating myocardial tissue deformation (i.e., lengthening, shortening, and thickening of the myocardium), historically by tissue Doppler but more recently via speckle-tracking echocardiography and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI).24Myocardial strain evaluation requires an averagedmeasurement of multiple myocardial segments along a given region and axis. During systole, myocardial contraction causes shortening of myocardial fibers in longitudinal axis (global longitudinal strain, GLS), myocardial thickening in the radial axis or short axis of the ventricle walls (radial strain), and circumferential contraction in the plane of the wall (circumferential strain). This results in a “wringing” effect as the heart’s apical region twists differentially than the base to propel blood out from the heart. Myocardial strain values are expressed as percent change. Right ventricular (RV) GLS, LVGLS, and circumferential strain are reported in negative values due to regional shortening; radial strain is reported as a positive value due to lengthening/thickening. Larger absolute values denote increased (enhanced) myocardial deformability, while smaller absolute valuesdenote decreased myocardial contractilityand systolic dysfunction.

Rationale for measuring myocardial strain in SCD

Myocardial strain can identify subclinical changes in ventricular systolic function that are undetectable using conventional echocardiographic measures such as LV ejection fraction (LVEF).Consequently, myocardial strain is an evolving marker of early cardiac disease with important prognostic value.25–28Abnormal myocardial strain is associated with increased morbidity and mortality, particularly in individuals with underlying cardiopulmonary disease.29, 30A growing number of studies have evaluated myocardial strain in individuals with SCD, but collective results have not yet been summarized.This systematic review is warranted tosynthesize reportedfindings and remaining gaps in knowledge.15, 31–47

Objectives

The objectives of our systematic review were to 1) identify all studies that have evaluatedventricular myocardial strain (RVGLS, LVGLS, LV circumferential strain,and LV radial strain) in individuals with SCD, 2) summarize values of myocardial strain in individuals with SCD and compare them with controls reported in the same studies, and 3) describe the echocardiographic techniques used to evaluate myocardial strain in SCD.

METHODS

To increase transparency and reproducibility, our review complies with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) reporting guidelines.48, 49

Protocol registration

Prior to starting the review, the study protocolwas published in Open Science Framework (OSF), aninternational database of prospectively registered systematic reviews, on April 3, 2020, and assigned the identifier: DOI 10.17605/OSF.IO/R29MW.

Eligibility criteria

Articles considered eligible for this systematic review met the following criteria: 1)included individuals with SCD (of any genotype), 2) were original articles published between January 1, 1965 andAugust 11, 2020, 3) evaluated RVGLS, LVGLS, LV circumferential strain, or LV radial strain via speckle-tracking echocardiography or other accepted echocardiographic method, 4) included observational, experimental, or quasi-experimental studies, including cohort studies, cross-sectional studies, randomized controlled trials, and case control studies, and 5) were written in English. Abstracts without corresponding manuscript, review articles, and case-reports or case-series wereexcluded.

Information sources and search strategy

We searched the following electronic bibliographic databases: Medline (Ovid) 1946 – 2020, Embase (embase.com) 1974 – 2020, Scopus (scopus.org) 1970 – 2020, and Web of Science (Clarivate Analytics) 1900 – 2020. No grey literature was searched. A medical librarian (T.M.) developed the primary search in Medline, which was peer-reviewed with the Peer Review of Electronic Search Strategies (PRESS) guidelines by an information specialist (M.M.M.) before being translated to the other databases.50The sensitive searches,customized to each database,included a combination of controlled vocabulary subject terms and keywords for each of our concepts: sickle cell disease, ventricular myocardial strain, and echocardiography. Our keywords were selected, by consensus of all authors, to capture all articles relevant to the studyobjectives.The final search strategies,originally run in April 2019, were updated on August 11, 2020(Supporting Information Table S1). Citation management and duplicate removal was accomplished with EndNote (Clarivate Analytics).

Study selection

All titles and abstracts of studies were independently screened by two reviewers (N.S.W., J.S.H.) to identify studies meeting the inclusion criteria. Full text eligibility was assessed by the same two reviewers. Discrepancies were resolved through discussion. Covidence (Covidence.org)was used to screen and review studies.51

Data collection and analysis

Data from the final selection of eligible articles wereindependently extracted by two review authors (N.S.W.,J.S.H.) using a standardized template that included these variables: number and age of subjects, sex, SCD genotype, heart rate, body surface area (BSA), hemoglobin concentration, myocardial strain (RVGLS, LVGLS, LV circumferential strain, and LV radial strain), LVEF and LV shortening fraction (LVSF), use of disease-modifying therapies for SCD, myocardial strain measurement technique,echocardiogram analysis software, and inclusion/exclusion criteria(Tables 1–3). To enhance our review, we contacted 5study authors for additional details not reported in published manuscripts.

Risk of bias quality assessment

Three review authors (N.S.W., J.S.H., V.G.N.) independently assessed the risk of bias in included studies by considering the following characteristics: 1) completeness of outcome data, 2) selective outcome reporting, and 3) other sources of bias.

RESULTS

Study selection

The PRISMA flow diagram is shown in Figure 1. A total of 836references were identified after 563duplicates were removed. All abstracts were reviewed by two review authors (N.S.W.,J.S.H.). A total of 794 records were excluded at title and abstract screening, leaving 42 eligible articles for full text review. We excluded 24 articles for incorrect outcome,ineligible study design, or duplicate (Supporting Information Table S2). Eighteen articles and 17studies met eligibility for study inclusion;Morissens et al published one article with preliminary findings.All results were summarized (Tables 1–3).15, 31–47

Study characteristics

The selected 17 studies were conducted in the United States (Ahmad et al, Coviet al, Whipple et al, Sachdev et al), Brazil (Barbosa et al, Braga et al,Bedirian et al, Resende et al), Egypt (Tolbaet al, AbdelMassihet al, Youssry et al), France (Blanc et al,Hammoudi et al), Portugal (Almeida et al), India (Sengupta et al), Belgium (Morissens et al), and Canada (Mah et al).Twelve of the studies were cross-sectional, three were prospective (Sengupta et al, Sachdev et al, Resende et al), one (Covi et al) was retrospective, and one (Mah et al) was experimental.15, 31–43, 45, 47Most studies obtained written consent and approval through an institutional review board or ethics committee. Eleven studies reported that patients were in steady state at the time of myocardial strain evaluation,defined as not being in any state of crisis or experiencing an acute event, generally for a minimum of one month.15, 32, 34–37, 39–42, 44, 47The remaining6 studies did not indicate timing of the patients’ last crisis in relation to myocardial strain evaluation (Table 3).31, 33, 37, 43, 45, 46Three studies(Covi et al, Sachdev et al, Sengupta et al) reported myocardial strain at multiple timepoints, but onlyvalues obtained at steady state were included in this systematic review.38, 43, 44In Covi et al and Sachdev et al, patients were undergoing hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT)myocardial strain values reported prior to transplantation(presumed steady state) were included.38, 43

All studies identified at least one inclusion or exclusion criteria.Exclusion criteria varied among studies but includedmany of thesame variables: cardiacdisease or dysfunction, pulmonary or systemic hypertension, recent acute crisis, kidney failure, recent surgery or blood transfusion, or significant comorbid medical conditions(Table 3).Seven studies defined an age requirement as an inclusion criterion: patients 5 – 18 years of age (Blanc et al),≥ 18 years of age (Hammoudi et al),18 – 40 years of age (Barbosa et al, Bedirian et al), ≥ 14 years of age (Braga et al), 6 months – 18 years of age(Tolba et al), and 5 – 19 years of age (Whipple et al) (Table 3).15, 34–37 39, 45

Patient characteristics

A total of 887 individuals with SCD across all genotypes were included in these17 studies. Thirteen studies reported sickle cell genotype with a combined total as follows: HbSS (n=648), HbSβ0thalassemia (n=25), HbSC (n=74), HbSβ+thalassemia (n=2), HbSβ thalassemia (n=1), and HbSD (n=1). All remaining subjects were classified as having SCD (n=136)(Table 1).15, 31–47Youssry et al classified patients by subphenotype, not genotype, as follows: viscosity vaso-occlusion cohort (lactate dehydrogenase < 270 U/L) andhemolysis endothelial dysfunction cohort (lactate dehydrogenase ≥ 270 U/L ).46Thirteen studies included controls as a comparison group for a combined total of421 controls (Table 1).15, 31–34, 36, 37, 39–41, 44–47Nine studies reported that controls were matched for age and gender, while4studies reported no significant difference in these variables.Eight studies evaluated children (age ≤ 17 years), and 12 evaluated adults. Five studies exclusively evaluated children, and 10 studies exclusively evaluated adults. The youngest patient was 6 monthsold, while the oldest patient was at least 55 years old.

Right ventricular global longitudinal strain

Seven of the 17 studies evaluated RVGLS. Mean RVGLS ranged from − 14.66 ± 4.25 to − 28.5 ± 5.6% in individuals with SCD and from − 18.08 ± 1.99 to − 34.1 ± 5.0% in controls.15, 34, 36,37, 42, 45, 46Five of these 7 studies evaluated children (age ≤ 17 years), and 5 evaluated adults (Table 1). Three studies evaluated RVGLS using a 3-segment method (free wall only), while 4 studies used a 6-segment method (free wall and septum) (Table 2). All studies evaluated myocardial strain via speckle-tracking echocardiography (Table 2).

Left ventricular global longitudinal strain

Fifteen of the 17 studies evaluated LVGLS, and 11 of them compared with controls. Mean LVGLS ranged from −14.3 ± 2.9 to − 22.5 ± 5.0% in individuals with SCD and from − 15.9 ± 2.8 to − 22.4 ± 2.8% in controls.15, 31–44, 47Six of these 15 studies evaluated children, and 12 evaluated adults (Table 1). Nine studies evaluated LVGLS using an 18-segment method, while 6-segment (n=2), 16-segment (n=2), and 17-segment (n=2) methods were used in other studies. Almeida et alevaluated myocardial strain via tissue Doppler imaging using only basal segments of all 6 walls; all other studies used speckle-tracking echocardiography (Table 2).33All studies used 2-dimensional echocardiography to assess LVGLS except for Ahmad et al which used 3-dimensional echocardiography. AbdelMassih et al and Sengupta et al evaluated LV epicardial and endocardial longitudinal strain (denoted subepicardial and subendocardial in Sengupta et al) (Table1).31, 44

Left ventricular circumferentialstrain

Seven of the 17 studies evaluated LV circumferential strain, and 6 of them compared with controls. Mean LV circumferential strain rangedfrom −18.4 ± 4.0 to − 24.4 ± 5.1% in individuals with SCD and from − 16.44 ± 3.6 to − 20.7 ± 1.4% in controls.32, 34, 36–38, 40, 44Four of these 7 studies evaluated children, and 5 evaluated adults (Table 1). Four studies evaluated LV circumferential strain using an 18-segment method, while 6-segment (n=1) and 16-segment (n=2) methods were used in other studies.All studies evaluated myocardial strain via speckle-tracking echocardiography (Table 2).Only Sengupta et al evaluated LV epicardial and endocardial circumferential strain (Table 1).44

Left ventricular radialstrain

Five of the 17 studies evaluated LV radial strain, and 4 of them compared with controls. Mean LV radial strain ranged from 27.11 ± 5.98to 78 ± 20% in individuals with SCD and from 21.97 ± 10.37 to 70 ± 21% in controls.32, 34, 37, 38, 44Two of these 5 studies evaluated children, and 4 evaluated adults (Table 1). Three studies evaluated LV radial strain using an 18-segment method, andtwo studies used a 16-segment method.All studies evaluated myocardial strain via speckle-tracking echocardiography (Table 2).

Myocardial strain in SCD compared with controls

Compared with controls, Almeida et al (p=0.004), Hammoudi et al (p=0.004), and Morissens et al (p<0.05) reported that individuals with SCD had decreased LVGLS. Tolba et al reported that individuals with SCD had decreased LV peak longitudinal strain (p=0.009). AbdelMassih et al reported that individuals with SCD had decreased LVGLS (p<0.01) and decreased LV endocardial longitudinal strain (p<0.01), while no significant difference was found in LV epicardial longitudinal strain. Ahmad et al reported that individuals with SCD had increased LVGLS (p<0.05), while no significant differences were found in LV circumferential or radial strain. Mah et al reported that individuals with SCD had increased LVGLS (p<0.05), while no significant difference was found in LV circumferential strain. Sengupta et al did not find any significant differences in LV epicardial or endocardial longitudinal or circumferential strain or in LV radial strain between the two cohorts (Table 1).

Blanc et al reported that individuals with SCD had decreased RVGLS (p=0.0003), while no significant differences were found in LVGLS or LV circumferential strain. Youssry et al reported that individuals with the hemolysis endothelial dysfunction subphenotype of SCD had decreased RVGLS (p<0.01). Whipple et al reported that individuals with SCD had increased RVGLS (p=0.005), while no significant difference was found in LVGLS. Neither Barbosa et al nor Braga el al found any significant differences in RVGLS, LVGLS, or LV circumferential or radial strain between the two cohorts. Covi et al, Bedirian et al, Sachdev et al, and Resende et al did not compare individuals with SCD to controls (Table 1).

Ejection fraction, shortening fraction, and TAPSE

Fifteen of the 17 studies evaluated either LVEF (n=13)or LVSF (n=2).15, 31–37, 39–44, 47Six studiesevaluated tricuspid annular plane systolicexcursion (TAPSE), which correlates closely with RVEF.15, 34, 36, 37, 42, 46, 52LVEF, LVSF, and TAPSE were normal in all studies andnot different among individuals with SCD andcontrols.

Disease-modifying therapy and age

Seven studies reported participants’ use of disease-modifying therapies (hydroxyurea or chronic transfusion therapy) at the time of evaluation, but only Whipple et al reported the difference in myocardial strain among subgroups. Barbosa et al and Whipple et al reported that RVGLS and LV myocardial strain were similar among individuals with SCD receiving hydroxyurea or chronic transfusion, individuals with SCD not receiving these therapies, and controls (Table 1).15, 32, 34, 36, 40–42, 47In a study of adults, Barbosa et al reported that LVGLS decreased with older age (β=0.104, p=0.009); in a study of children, Whipple et al reported that RVGLS decreased with older age (ρ=0.338, p<0.001).15, 34

DISCUSSION

Summary of evidence

Until August 11, 2020, 17 studies have evaluated myocardial strain in individuals with SCD;this systematic review summarizes their results. Moststudies identified statistically significant differences in RVGLS and LVGLS, but not in LV circumferential or radial strain, between individuals with SCD and controls(Table 1).

LVGLS

Almeida et al, Hammoudi et al, AbdelMassih et al, and Morissens et al reported that individuals with SCD had significantly decreased LVGLS compared with controls, while Ahmad et aland Mah et al reported an increased LVGLS.31–33, 39, 41, 47This discrepancy may be due in part to the fact that Ahmad et al and Mah et al’s control groups had a much lowerLVGLS than other control groups and also a lower LVGLS than published reference values for a cohort of similar age.53Additionally, Ahmad et al was the only study to measure myocardial strain using 3-dimensional echocardiography. These studies lacked a uniform myocardial strain evaluation technique. Although 16-segment, 17-segment, and 18-segment methods for evaluating LV myocardial strain are all accepted techniques, they divide the myocardium differently at the apical level and make direct comparison problematic.52, 54Although each method may have slightly varying benefits and limitations for the indication and population being assessed, the 18-segment method has been the most widely reported method for assessing function among individuals with SCD; standardization at an institutional level is recommended.52, 54, 55Using a 6-segment method to evaluate LVGLS is not standard. Both 2- and 3-dimensional echocardiography are accepted techniques for measuring myocardial strain but are not interchangeable.55 Similarly, tissue Doppler and speckle-tracking echocardiography are not interchangeable methods. Barbosa et al and Whipple et al evaluated the two largest SCD cohorts compared with controls. Both studied a homogenous patient population with severe SCD genotype, used an 18-segment method, and excluded patients in crisis at the time of evaluation. Their finding of no difference in LVGLS may be a more significant and reproducible result compared with smaller studies that evaluated more heterogenous patient populations with more confounding variables. 15, 34

Hammoudi et al reported that LVGLS decreased with increasing LV hypertrophy and reasoned that hypertrophy and myocardial fibrosis led to decreased LV function.Morissens et al found no association between increased LVGLS and clinical or biological parametersandrecommended that larger studies be performed to avoid conflicting results that often arise from small cohort studies. Ahmad et al reasoned that LVGLS was increased due to compensatory mechanisms within the cardiovascular system aimed at maintaining adequate oxygenation in anemic patients.

RVGLS

Blanc et al, Tolba et al, and Youssry et al reported that individuals with SCD had decreased RVLGS, while Whipple et al reported an increased RVGLScompared with controls (Table 1).15, 36, 45, 46Although both a 3-segment method (free wall only) and a 6-segment method (free wall and septum) are accepted techniques for evaluating RVGLS, emerging data suggest that the 6-segment method is a more robust and reproducible technique.Additionally, these two methods are not interchangeable, as the 3-segment method results in a higher absolute value. 52, 55, 56 Because Blanc et al and Youssry et al measured RVGLS using a 3-segment method, it is difficult to make direct comparisons with Whipple et al, which used a 6-segment method. Youssry et al did not identify what SCD genotypes were evaluated, further confounding the comparison. Tolba et al reported RVGLS for the youngest cohort in our review, included infants, and did not identify SCD genotype. These differences make comparisons challenging, but may further support a conclusion that Whipple et al’s finding of increased RVGLS may be a more significant and reproducible result. Blanc et al reported that RVGLS decreased with increasing pulmonary pressure (measured by tricuspid regurgitant velocity). Youssry et al reported that RVGLS decreased with increasing lactate dehydrogenase values and reasoned that hemolysis led to microvascular dysfunction and subendocardial ischemia. However, Whipple et alevaluated both of these variables and found no association with RVGLS. Whipple et al studied a much larger SCD cohort and control group than all other studiesand suggested that elevated RVGLS may indicate an early enhancement of cardiac systolic function as a compensatory response to preceding insults to the myocardium and increased cardiac output (Table 1, Table 2). Whipple et al also demonstrated that RVGLS decreases with age,left atrial dilation, and diastolic dysfunction.15

Variables affecting myocardial strain

Age, sex, blood pressure, and evaluation technique contribute to variabilityin published normal values of myocardial strain in healthy children and adults.53, 57–59Other variableslikely also affect myocardial strain evaluation in individuals with SCD by altering cardiovascular and hyperdynamic physiology. These variables include: interval since last blood transfusion or acute crisis event; frequency and severity of previous acute and chronic cardiopulmonary complications (e.g., acute chest syndrome and pulmonary hypertension);diastolic dysfunction; cardiac loading conditions and high-cardiac output statefrom chronic anemia; and sickle cell genotype, which significantlyaffects disease onset and severity.Variability in these covariates, often significant, exists among these 17 studies, which makes myocardial strain interpretation and comparison difficult. Age and disease severity in SCD likely play a particularly significant role in myocardial strain evaluation.15, 53, 57–59 To date, few studies have evaluated myocardial strain in middle to older aged individuals with SCD, which could prove critical to understanding thenatural evolution and interpretation of this functional marker.

Evaluation technique

Presently, the technology and training to measure strain by speckle-tracking echocardiography is more readily availableand tested. However, myocardial strain evaluation by MRI is an emerging technique, which may provide particular benefit inindividualswith poor acoustic windows.57, 60

Recommendations

Although screening echocardiograms are not currently recommended in asymptomatic individuals with SCD, evaluation of cardiac function via echocardiogram may provide actionable data in patients with repeated cardiopulmonary complicationsandin hospitalized patients with cardiopulmonary insults.14, 61, 62Becausemyocardial strain abnormalities are often present when other conventional echocardiographic parameters of cardiac function(e.g. LVEF) are preserved, we recommend consideration of myocardial strain analysis, especially RVGLS and LVGLS, any time an echocardiogram is performed in individuals with SCD.5, 6, 8–12Such evaluation may allow early detection of subclinical changes incardiac function and identify opportunities for early medicalinterventions or increased surveillance.

Limitations

The reviewed studies had several methodological limitations. Twelveof the 17 studies reported small sample size as a study limitation, with9 studies evaluating study cohorts of ≤ 30 subjects.32, 33, 35, 36, 38–41, 44–47Small sample sizes make it difficult to generalize results to the larger SCD population.Variability in SCD genotype among studies makes myocardial strain comparisons difficult to interpret.15, 31, 32, 34–36, 38, 41–43, 45–47Individuals with SCD undergoing HSCT likely had a more severe disease burden, which may have introduced selection bias.38, 43In Sachdev et al, 19 patients were taking cardiovascular medications at the time of myocardial strain assessment, which was an exclusion criteria for at least one study (Table 3).43Variability in genotype and disease severity may threaten external validity. Most studies did not identify how study participants or controls were selected, which introduces possible selection bias. There was limited reporting of and significant variability in inclusion/exclusion criteria as well as variability in how steady state was defined; these differencesmay affect external validity (Table 3). No instances of selective outcome reporting were identified.

Conclusions

RVGLS and LVGLS are generally abnormal in individuals with SCD when compared with controls and may serve as an early marker of altered myocardial function in this population. RVGLS and LVGLS are most commonly reported to be decreased in individuals with SCD.However, several studies found no difference in LVGLS, including Barbosa et al and Whipple et al, which evaluated the two largest SCD cohorts compared with controls. Whipple et alfound that RVGLS was increased in individuals with SCDand may indicate an early RV compensatory mechanism to SCD-related cardiac abnormalities. Myocardial strain analysis should be considered any time an echocardiogram is performed in individuals with SCD. Prospective longitudinal studies with long-term follow-up across all SCD genotypes and age groups, using large cohorts adjusted for SCD-specific covariates, are necessary to better characterizemyocardial strain in SCD and to determine whether it canserve as an early marker of altered myocardial function.
