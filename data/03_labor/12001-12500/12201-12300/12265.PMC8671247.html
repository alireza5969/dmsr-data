Wide-area transepithelial sampling for dysplasia detection in Barrett’s esophagus: a systematic review and meta-analysis

Abstract

Background and Aims:

Seattle protocol forceps biopsies (FB) are currently recommended for surveillance in Barrett’s esophagus (BE) but limited by sampling error and lack of adherence. Wide-area transepithelial sampling with 3D analysis (WATS-3D) is reported to increase BE dysplasia detection. We assessed the incremental yield and clinical significance of WATS-3D for dysplasia detection over FB in a systematic review and meta-analysis.

Methods:

We queried major scientific databases for studies using WATS-3D and FB from 2000–2020. Primary outcome was the incremental yield of WATS-3D detected dysplasia (defined as a composite of indefinite for dysplasia [IND], low- and high-grade dysplasia [LGD, HGD] and esophageal adenocarcinoma [EAC]) over FB. Secondary outcomes were incremental yields of HGD/EAC, and rate of reconfirmation of WATS-3D dysplasia on subsequent FB.

Results:

Meta-analysis of 7 eligible studies demonstrated that FB diagnosed dysplasia in 15.9% of cases, whereas incremental yield with WATS-3D was 7.2% (95% CI, 3.9%−11.5%; I2=92.1%). Meta-analysis of 6 studies demonstrated that FB diagnosed HGD/EAC in 2.3% of patients, whereas incremental yield with WATS-3D was 2.1% (95% CI, 0.4%−5.3%; I2=92.7%). Notably, WATS-3D was negative in 62.5% of cases where FB identified dysplasia. Two studies reported reconfirmation of WATS-3D dysplasia with FB histology in only 20 patients.

Conclusion:

WATS-3D increases dysplasia detection, however, the clinical significance of this increased dysplasia detection remains uncertain. Data from endoscopic follow up to ascertain FB histology in patients with dysplasia based solely on WATS-3D are needed to determine the optimal clinical application and significance of WATS only dysplasia.

Introduction

The incidence of esophageal adenocarcinoma (EAC), a lethal disease when diagnosed after the onset of symptoms, has risen rapidly over the last 4 decades.1–3 Endoscopic and surgical therapies have demonstrated improved survival in patients diagnosed with localized disease.4–6 Barrett’s esophagus (BE) is the only known precursor lesion of EAC,7 and progression risk increases in patients with dysplasia.8–11 Hence, gastroenterological societies have advocated for screening and surveillance, with the aim of intervening with endoscopic therapy in patients with early stage EAC or high-grade dysplasia (HGD) and low-grade dysplasia (LGD).12–14

Guidelines recommend meticulous examination of BE segments and targeted biopsies of mucosal abnormalities, and utilization of the Seattle biopsy protocol.14 However, adherence with this protocol is low.15 Even strict adherence is subject to sampling bias, as the majority of BE mucosa will not be sampled.16 The vast majority of EACs are diagnosed outside surveillance programs,17 despite evidence that EACs diagnosed in surveillance may have improved cancer related mortality.18,1920 Moreover, the rates of “missed” dysplasia and EAC remain high, with up to a quarter of all EAC being “missed.”21 This suggests that there are substantial gaps in our current surveillance practices.

Wide Area Transepithelial Sampling with Three-Dimensional Analysis (WATS-3D; CDx Diagnostics, Suffern, NY, USA) is a novel technology that offers the possibility of reducing sampling error by allowing sampling of a higher proportion of the BE mucosa using endoscopic brushings followed by neural network aided dysplasia detection by trained pathologists. Multiple studies have suggested that the utilization of WATS-3D may increase dysplasia yield by 40% over conventional surveillance.22, 23 Despite inclusion in a recent guideline as a potential adjunct to Seattle protocol biopsies,24 several issues have limited the widespread adoption of WATS-3D in clinical practice. Hence, we aimed to summarize the incremental dysplasia yield of WATS-3D by systematic review and meta-analysis, in particular highlighting the adjunctive yield of adding WATS-3D to traditional endoscopic FB surveillance, assessing the rate of incongruent biopsy results, and most importantly the FB histologic follow-up data on WATS-3D only detected dysplastic BE (DBE).

Methods

This systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted in accordance with PRISMA guidelines (Figure 1).25

WATS-3D technique and interpretation

For sample acquisition, an abrasive brush is passed through the biopsy channel of the endoscope to sample a large circumferential area of the BE segment, and a transepithelial sample of the esophageal mucosa up to the lamina propria is obtained (this may also contain thin mucosal strip biopsies). These brushing samples are then placed on slides, stained, and analyzed using a proprietary neural network assisted 3-dimensional computer algorithm trained to highlight areas of irregularity such as abnormal cells, glands, and cell clusters. The algorithm then flags 80 of the most suspicious images for interpretation by pathologists, who are specially trained to interpret WATS-3D images.26, 27

Study Selection

Studies eligible for inclusion were prospective in design and conducted on human subjects. Case series/reports and expert opinion/commentary were excluded, as were conference abstracts given the expected significant overlap in patients as WATS-3D databases contributed to multiple studies. This strategy ensured that no patients would be double counted in our analysis. Articles included in the meta-analysis had to report at least one of the outcomes of interest, including (1) the increase in dysplasia diagnosis made by WATS-3D in comparison to FB, (2) the increase in HGD/EAC diagnosis made by WATS-3D in comparison to FB, (3) the rates of discordant diagnoses made by WATS-3D and FB, and (4) subsequent histological outcomes of WATS-3D dysplasia as assessed by follow-up FB.

Search Strategy and Data Abstraction

We conducted a comprehensive search of the scientific literature by querying the major databases Ovid MEDLINE, Cochrane CENTRAL, SCOPUS, Web of Science, PubMed, and Ovid EMBASE from January 2000 to December 2020, using the keywords: Barrett esophagus, esophageal adenocarcinoma, dysplasia, wide area transepithelial sampling with 3-dimensional analysis, and progression. Reference lists of analyzed articles were also examined to assess for additional articles for inclusion. The detailed search strategy is included in the supplementary appendix.

Two authors (DCC and AKC) independently assessed each title and abstract that resulted from the search strategy, and articles that met our inclusion criteria were further reviewed in full text independently. Disagreements between the 2 data abstractors were resolved by consensus upon consultation with a senior board-certified gastroenterologist (PGI). When data were not extractable or were unclear, we attempted to contact the authors for further clarification.

The following data were abstracted from the included articles:

  1. Author, year, and place where the study was conducted,

  2. Study period,

  3. Funding source for the study,

  4. Demographics,

  5. Description of pathologist experience and source interpreting WATS-3D and FB samples,

  6. Number of congruent dysplastic and non-dysplastic BE cases by both WATS-3D and FB,

  7. Number of discordant dysplastic and non-dysplastic BE cases by WATS-3D in comparison to FB,

  8. Order of testing algorithm (FB first, then WATS-3D or vice versa),

  9. Number of patients who progressed to EAC/HGD based on WATS-3D and FB diagnosis,

  10. Number of patients who died due to any cause after receiving a diagnosis of HGD/EAC,

  11. Stage of cancers at the time of diagnosis,

  12. Cox-proportional hazard ratios (HR) or risk ratios (RR) and confidence intervals (CI) for mortality. Both adjusted HRs and unadjusted HRs were extracted from individual studies.

Outcomes Assessed in the Meta-Analysis

Articles from the systematic review were eligible for quantitative synthesis whenever outcome data were extractable, and the data were amenable to be combined in the form of a meta-analysis. Our primary outcome of interest was the incremental yield of WATS-3D over FB for the diagnosis of dysplasia, defined as new cases of DBE identified by WATS-3D alone but negative for dysplasia by FB. A composite outcome incorporating indefinite for dysplasia (IND), LGD, HGD, and EAC was used as the endpoint for this outcome. Crypt dysplasia was excluded as an outcome in our analysis. Secondary outcomes included 1. The incremental yield of WATS-3D in the diagnosis of HGD/EAC 2. Miss rate of WATS (FB + but WATS − results), 3. Reconfirmation of WATS-3D dysplasia results on subsequent FB samples when the initial result was positive on WATS-3D but negative on FB 4. Additional time taken for acquiring WATS samples 5. Adverse events from WATS and, 6. Progression to HGD/EAC and mortality (both EAC specific and all-cause) for patients diagnosed with WATS-3D only dysplasia compared with FB alone.

We also planned subgroup analyses a priori to examine the incremental yield of WATS-3D over FB for the diagnosis of DBE and HGD/EAC in surveillance populations (as opposed to combined screening and surveillance populations in the primary outcome analysis).

Assessment of Individual Study Data

We used the Newcastle-Ottawa scale (NOS) for cohort studies to review individual study quality.28 The NOS assessed the 3 domains of selection (4 questions), comparability (2 questions), and ascertainment of outcome (3 questions). A maximum score of 9 could be awarded to a study. We modified the NOS suitably to reflect the objectives of our study and outcomes of interest highlighting the following aspects: (1) attempt at reconfirmation of WATS-3D biopsy results by FB on subsequent surveillance, (2) adherence to currently advocated Seattle protocol biopsies in the FB group, (3) FB and WATS pathology interpreted by independent expert GI pathologists, and (4) whether long-term follow-up was planned a priori for DBE patients diagnosed using WATS-3D.

Data Analysis

Given the currently accepted use of WATS-3D as an adjunct to, and not in place of FB, we used a single-arm meta-analysis of proportions to determine the incremental yield of WATS-3D biopsies in conjunction with FB. Binary data were abstracted from included studies. The numerator was the number of patients positive on WATS-3D but negative on FB. The denominator consisted of all patients with BE on FB, regardless of WATS-3D diagnosis. We used the arc-sine transformation with a random-effects model given the expected heterogeneity among studies included in the meta-analysis (due to differences in study populations and sample sizes). OpenMeta[Analyst] (Brown University, Providence, Rhode Island, USA) was used to perform all meta-analyses and produce corresponding forest plots.29 Heterogeneity between studies was assessed using the I2 statistic, and I2 values of 25%, 50%, and 75% were deemed to represent low, moderate, and high heterogeneity.30 We did not use funnel plots for reporting publication bias, given the expected paucity of WATS-3D studies.

Results

Our initial search strategy yielded 204 studies, with 7 articles eligible for inclusion (Figure 1).22, 23, 26, 31–34 Basic study characteristics are provided in Table 1 and study specific highlights are provided in Supplementary table 1. These studies included 18,842 patients (of which 15,636 patients tested negative for BE on FB and WATS-3D yielding 3,206 patients diagnosed with BE based on FB alone, who were used in the analyses of this study; 302 patients were diagnosed with DBE based on FB and details are shown in supplementary table 2). One trial was randomized in regard to whether FB occurred before WATS-3D or vice versa,26 in 3 trials WATS-3D brushings were obtained before FB,22, 23, 33 whereas in 2 trials, FB were obtained before WATS-3D brushings.32, 34 In one trial, endoscopists were instructed to alternate the order of sampling.31 All WATS samples were read only by pathologists at CDx Diagnostics (Suffern, NY, USA) except one study wherein discordant results were confirmed by pathologists at Cleveland Clinic. CDx Diagnostics provided funding support for 5 of 7 studies.22, 23, 26, 31, 33

Primary Outcome: Incremental dysplasia (composite of IND, LGD, HGD, EAC) yield of WATS-3D over FB

Seven total studies were eligible for inclusion in this outcome with 3,206 patients.22, 23, 26, 31–34 Meta-analysis demonstrated that FB diagnosed dysplasia in 15.9% (95% CI, 5.4%−30.5%; I2 = 98.7%; Supplementary figure 1) of cases, whereas WATS-3D led to an incremental yield of 7.2% (95% CI, 3.9%−11.5%; I2=92.1%; Figure 2).

Secondary outcome: Incremental yield of WATS-3D over FB for the diagnosis of HGD/EAC

Six total studies were eligible for inclusion in this outcome with 2,839 patients.23, 26, 31–34 Meta-analysis of these studies demonstrated that FB diagnosed HGD/EAC in 2.3% (95% CI, 0.6%−5.1%; I2=90.1%; supplementary figure 2) of patients, whereas WATS-3D added an incremental yield of 2.1% (95% CI, 0.4%−5.3%; I2=92.7%; Figure 3).

Secondary outcome: WATS-3D false negative rate and confirmation of WATS-3D results by biopsy

WATS-3D was negative in 62.5% of cases where FB identified dysplasia (145 of 232 in 4 articles where this specific data were able to be parsed out).22, 23, 27, 33

Only 2 23, 26 out of 7 studies included in this manuscript reported reconfirmation of WATS-3D dysplasia on subsequent endoscopy. In one study,26 9 of 23 (40%) WATS-3D only HGD/EAC (+) patients (FB −), had endoscopic re-evaluation of dysplasia with FB. Of these 9 patients, HGD/EAC was confirmed on subsequent FB in 4 patients (44%), 3 had LGD and 2 had no dysplasia. The remaining 14 patients had undergone ablation (11) or were lost to follow-up (3). The exact timing of these follow-up endoscopies was not reported in this study.26 In another study, a post-hoc analysis with follow up ranging from 3 to 18 months after study inclusion demonstrated that 5 out of 11 patients with WATS-3D dysplasia had a dysplasia diagnosis confirmed on follow up endoscopy with FB.23 In the remaining studies, there was no reconfirmation of WATS +/FB − dysplasia with follow-up endoscopic assessment. There were no long-term follow-up studies of WATS only dysplasia published as manuscripts.

In 4 studies the incremental yield of WATS −3D dysplasia was reported in several cases with FB histology negative for intestinal metaplasia but WATS-3D positive for goblet cells.22, 31, 33, 34 No endoscopic data were available in these cases to assess if these patients truly had BE.

Secondary Outcome: Time Required to Complete WATS-3D Brushings

Only one study reported the excess time required to obtain brush biopsies for WATS-3D interpretation.26 The addition of WATS-3D brushings increased procedure time by an average of 4 minutes 31 seconds, in addition to the 6 minutes and 55 seconds required for obtaining Seattle protocol biopsies (range/standard deviation not reported).

Secondary outcome: adverse events from WATS-3D

In 3 studies comprising 17,210 patients that reported on complications or adverse events, no adverse events were recorded in either the FB or WATD-3D groups.31, 33, 34

Secondary Outcome: Exclusion of WATS-3D Positive IND from total pool of DBE

Five studies comprising 1,067 patients provided granular detail allowing for the exclusion of IND cases from the outcome of WATS-3D positive DBE.23, 32–34 In these studies, meta-analysis demonstrated that FB diagnosed DBE in 12.4% (95% CI, 3.6%−25.6%; I2=96.1; supplementary figure 3) of patients, whereas WATS-3D added an incremental yield of 7.3% (95% CI, 3.3%−12.8%; I2=86.3%; supplementary figure 4).

Secondary Outcome: Progression rates to HGD/EAC and mortality (both EAC specific and all-cause) for patients diagnosed with WATS-3D only dysplasia compared with FB alone

No studies meeting a priori inclusion criteria for this systematic review reported outcomes related to progression rates or mortality. However, we identified one study in abstract form that provided data on progression rates (to HGD/EAC) after the diagnosis of WATS-3D dysplasia.35 Notably, these progression rates were from a subsequent diagnosis of HGD/EAC based only on WATS-3D, and not FB. In this study, of 4,049 NDBE patients, 19 progressed to HGD/EAC (diagnosed on WATS-3D only), leading to a progression rate of 0.33% per patient-year (median of 1.4 years of follow up per patient) in those with baseline WATS showing either no dysplasia or LGD.

No studies have reported the EAC related mortality with WATS-3D diagnosis of HGD/EAC.

Subgroup analyses:

Patients in BE surveillance only:

Although the prior analysis (for the primary outcome) included all patients with intestinal metaplasia on FB, in this group only patients with a prior history of intestinal metaplasia on a previous endoscopy with histologic confirmation of this diagnosis (ie, those with confirmed BE in surveillance) were included. Meta-analysis of these 6 studies with 1,609 patients 22, 23, 31–33 demonstrated that although FB diagnosed dysplasia (any grade) in 19.4% (95% CI, 4.8%−40.9%; I2=98.8%; supplementary figure 5) of cases, WATS-3D led to an incremental dysplasia yield of 6.9% (95% CI, 3.0%−12.3%; I2=91.8%; supplementary figure 6).

Five studies included information regarding the diagnosis of HGD/EAC in this subset of patients.23, 26, 31–33 Meta-analysis of these 5 studies with 1,391 patients demonstrated that FB diagnosed dysplasia in 2.8% (95% CI, 0.5%−6.8%; I2=90.6%; supplementary figure 7) of cases, whereas WATS-3D added an incremental HGD/EAC yield of 2.4% (95% CI, 0.2%−7.0%; I2=93.1%; supplementary figure 8).

Quality Assessment:

A modified version of the Newcastle-Ottawa scale was used for evaluation of study quality,28 and quality characteristics are shown in supplementary table 3. No study was considered to be of “high quality” (score ≥ 7), whereas 4 studies were considered to be moderate quality (scores between 5 and 6),23,26, 34,32 and the remaining studies were rated as poor (scores between 0 and 4).22, 27, 33

Discussion:

Given the significant rate of “missed HGD/EAC” in BE surveillance, WATS-3D may be useful by sampling a greater surface area of the esophageal mucosa along with computer aided dysplasia detection. In this study, we found that WATS-3D increased the absolute yield of dysplastic BE (all grades including IND) and HGD/EAC over conventional FB by 7.2% and 2.1%, respectively. This must be interpreted within the context of most studies lacking reconfirmation of WATS-3D only dysplasia by subsequent endoscopic FB. Additionally, most of the incremental dysplasia yield from WATS-3D reflects LGD diagnosed on WATS-3D alone (as exclusion of IND in subset analysis did not significantly alter WATS-3D dysplasia yield). Additionally, WATS-3D was negative in 62.5% of cases where FB detected dysplasia. Hence it likely remains an adjunctive technique to endoscopic surveillance with FB at this time.

Endoscopic surveillance as currently performed results in a high rate of missed dysplasia.21 Multiple factors may explain this, such as limited sampling of the BE mucosa by FB, leading to sampling error, lack of compliance with recommended Seattle protocol biopsies,15 lack of recognition of subtle mucosal abnormalities, and the focal distribution of dysplasia in BE.36 WATS-3D samples a greater proportion of the BE mucosa followed by a more efficient analysis of the sample and this is plausible reason for increased detection of dysplasia with WATS.

However, several issues need to be considered. The technique and criteria for assessment of dysplasia by WATS-3D differs from the assessment by FB. Most WATS-3D dysplasia diagnoses in published studies were not re-confirmed by FB in those with discordant results. Two studies reported that a minority (30%−45.5%) of WATS-3D only dysplasia were confirmed on subsequent endoscopic surveillance.23,26 Another consideration is the possibility of overdiagnosis of dysplasia in WATS-3D specimens, just as it is possible to overdiagnose dysplasia in endoscopic biopsy specimens. WATS-3D specimens by their design result in deeper sampling of the mucosa. It is well known that basally located glands in a BE segment always show some degree of cytologic atypia, which is considered “baseline atypia” at the base of a segment of Barrett’s mucosa.37, 38 In biopsy specimens, surface maturation is easily seen, and this “baseline atypia” can be interpreted in context. However, in the absence of these architectural features in WATS specimens, it is possible to overdiagnose basally located glands as dysplastic. This is further complicated by the issue of so-called “crypt dysplasia” because, by definition, crypt dysplasia shows surface maturation.39 Although crypt dysplasia can be difficult to reproducibly diagnose on forceps biopsy specimens, this diagnosis is even more difficult in a WATS-3D specimen, which lacks surface orientation. Additionally, almost all WATS-3D pathology has been read by 4 pathologists employed by CDx limiting generalizability to pathologists not trained in this technology. In only one study were the discordant WATS-3D only positive dysplastic slides blindly reviewed by 2 expert GI pathologists from Cleveland Clinic.26 Criteria for the diagnosis for dysplasia on WATS-3D have also not been clearly defined or published. Although one study did report on good interobserver agreement among 4 pathologists, all 4 were employed by CDx.40 WATS-3D brush samples are reported to mimic “mini” biopsy specimens because of deeper sampling of the epithelium.27 Hence, glandular morphology, which is missing from standard cytology brushings, might provide improved dysplasia assessment. However, there is minimal lamina propria and muscularis mucosae in WATS-3D epithelial specimens to assess, the presence of which can be key for differentiating HGD from invasive cancer. Architectural features (which are an element of assessment for columnar neoplasia) also cannot be evaluated, unlike in standard biopsy specimens.41, 42

Finally, the natural history of patients diagnosed with WATS-3D only dysplasia remains largely unknown. It is unclear if these would differ from progression rates for patients with FB dysplasia. Unfortunately, the studies included in this meta-analysis did not provide this important information. A recent abstract by Shaheen et al35 provided follow-up data on 83 patients who had LGD on WATS-3D and found 8 patients progressed to HGD/EAC based on subsequent WATS-3D sampling, (at a rate of 7.7% per patient year), which may mirror previously reported rates based on FB.35 A full manuscript is awaited. Last, it is also important to note that in 4 of the published WATS studies, FB diagnosed dysplasia was not read by a pathologist with expertise in gastrointestinal pathology.

Two other meta-analyses on WATS-3D deserve mention, namely the recent ASGE guideline on BE, and another meta-analysis by Kumar et al.24, 43 Both included conference abstracts. Neither meta-analysis included the studies by Raphael et al32 and Agha et al,34 which we included. Our meta-analysis has several distinct differences when compared with the 2 other meta-analyses. We only included published articles in our meta-analysis given concern that there is overlap in participants between several of the conference abstracts and the published articles. Second, in both previously published meta-analyses, a traditional paired meta-analysis was performed. However, in most studies, FB was sequentially followed by WATS-3D in the same population and these procedures were not directly compared with each other in distinct population groups. Moreover, WATS-3D is currently considered an adjunct to, and is not meant to replace FB surveillance, hence we felt that the data were more appropriate to be analyzed as a single-arm meta-analysis of proportions. We also included important subgroup analyses, such as excluding patients with IND, and analyzing HGD/EAC results separately, which are clinically more important outcomes.

Our study has several strengths, including the use of rigid methodological criteria in assessing eligible studies and the comprehensive literature search. There are potential limitations in our study. The small number of included studies (which reflects the published literature) limited our ability to assess for publication bias. Study quality was suboptimal. Published studies have reported that community pathologists routinely overcall LGD, and in 4 of 7 studies it was not clear whether interpretation of FB dysplasia was done by expert gastrointestinal pathologists.44, 45 This is critical because a majority of the WATS incremental yield was LGD. In the absence of reported BE segment length in many of the included cases, it is possible that endoscopists using the WATS-3D brushes may have brushed the cardia rather than true BE in the tubular esophagus. Last, there was substantial heterogeneity noted in our meta-analysis. However, this was not surprising as high heterogeneity is common in single arm meta-analyses given that the this stems from differences in the magnitude and not direction of effects.

In conclusion, we found in a systematic review and meta-analysis that WATS-3D appears to increase the yield of dysplasia when used as an adjunct to FB, in particular substantially increasing the yield of IND/LGD, without in most cases being confirmed by subsequent endoscopic FB biopsies. Almost all WATS-3D pathology was interpreted by CDx Diagnostics pathologists. Because there are no long-term follow-up studies, outcomes of dysplasia diagnosed based solely on WATS-3D remain unclear and need to be defined. Cost effectiveness of adding WATS-3D as an adjunct to endoscopic surveillance also needs to be assessed. Data on these important issues need to be obtained and analyzed to truly ascertain the value of incorporating WATS-3D in BE surveillance.
