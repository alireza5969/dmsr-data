Abstract

Background and Aims

Intestinal strictures are a common complication of Crohn’s disease (CD). Biomarkers of intestinal strictures would assist in their prediction, diagnosis, and monitoring. Herein we provide a comprehensive systematic review of studies assessing biomarkers that may predict or diagnose CD-associated strictures.

Methods

We performed a systematic review of PubMed, EMBASE, ISI Web of Science, Cochrane Library, and Scopus to identify citations pertaining to biomarkers of intestinal fibrosis through July 6, 2020 that used a reference standard of full thickness histopathology and/or cross-sectional imaging and/or endoscopy. Studies were categorized based upon the type of biomarker they evaluated (serum, genetic, histopathologic, or fecal).

Results

Thirty-five distinct biomarkers from three major groups were identified: serum (20 markers), genetic (9 markers) and histopathology (6 markers). Promising markers include cartilage oligomeric matrix protein, hepatocyte growth factor activator, and lower levels of microRNA-19-3p (AUC 0.805, 0.738 and 0.67 respectively), and multiple anti-flagellin antibodies (A4-Fla2 [OR 3.41], anti Fla-X [OR 2.95], and anti-CBir1 [multiple]). Substantial heterogeneity was observed and none of the markers had undergone formal validation. Specific limitations to acceptance of these markers included failure to use a standardized definition of stricturing disease, lack of specificity, and insufficient relevance to the pathogenesis of intestinal strictures or incomplete knowledge regarding their operating properties.

Conclusions

There is a lack of well-defined studies on biomarkers of intestinal stricture. Development of reliable and accurate biomarkers of stricture is a research priority. Biomarkers can support the clinical management of CD patients and aid in the stratification and monitoring of patients during clinical trials of future antifibrotic drug candidates.

Graphical Abstract

INTRODUCTION

The development of intestinal fibrosis and strictures is a common complication for patients with Crohn’s disease (CD). Despite the increasing armamentarium of anti-inflammatory medications, the rate of stricture formation in CD remains high.1, 2 Furthermore, the stricturing disease phenotype is linked to high morbidity, hospitalizations, need for surgery, and poor quality of life.1–3 The future potential for a patient without complications to develop a stricturing phenotype is a major consideration that influences treatment decisions by clinicians. Despite existing CD guidelines,4–7 currently the choice and timing of therapy are based exclusively upon clinical presentation of disease. The ability to use biomarkers to accurately predict which patients will develop intestinal strictures would be a powerful patient management tool enabling clinicians to adjust the long-term therapy according to risk stratification. Due to the substantial contribution of fibrosis to intestinal strictures, a biomarker may also serve as a surrogate for the ‘fibrotic burden’, which would facilitate clinical trials in fibrostenosing CD by defining high risk populations or tracking improvement in fibrogenesis, or with appropriate validation it may serve as a clinical trial endpoint.6, 8–10

Given these considerations, the development of biomarkers for intestinal fibrostenosis resulting in strictures is a research priority. As a first step in advancing a comprehensive biomarker program driven by the Stenosis Therapy and Anti-Fibrotic Research (STAR) Consortium,8–10 we performed a systematic literature review to summarize available biomarkers for the prediction and diagnosis of stricturing CD.

METHODS

We conducted a systematic review using a pre-specified protocol, based on the 2009 Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines.11 The protocol was registered on PROSPERO, registration ID: CRD42019139421. A systematic search of PubMed, EMBASE, ISI Web of Science, Cochrane Library, and Scopus was conducted to identify citations pertaining to biomarkers of CD associated strictures up until July 6, 2020.

Inclusion criteria: CD, stricture must have been evaluated as an independent disease characteristic separate from inflammation or penetrating/fistulizing disease; only adult (≥18 years of age) human subjects; investigation of a serum, stool, body fluid, histopathologic, or genetic biomarker; biomarker must demonstrate an association with diagnosis or prediction of stricturing disease. The presence of an accepted reference standard of full thickness histopathology and/or cross-sectional imaging and/or endoscopy was required.

Exclusion criteria: Studies only available in abstract form; non-English language; less than a total of three patients; animal studies; no evaluation of stricturing CD; non CD causes of intestinal stricture, such as radiation, NSAIDs or other; ileal pouch anal anastomosis and other anastomotic non CD small bowel strictures; review articles; all studies that do not use full thickness histopathology and/or cross-sectional imaging and/or endoscopy as the reference standard for all patients included in the respective manuscript (endoscopic mucosal biopsies or need for surgery for stenotic disease as a surrogate means to diagnose or quantify stricture was not acceptable). Cross sectional imaging was not evaluated as a potential biomarker or predictor of stricture as this topic has been previously addressed.8

The remainder of the methods can be found in Supplemental Methods and the PRISMA diagram can be found in Supplemental Figure 1.

RESULTS

The literature search identified a total of 7,003 citations. Forty-eight papers met inclusion criteria and 35 distinct potential biomarkers were identified. These were subdivided into four major groups: serum (20 markers), genetic (9 markers) as well as histopathology or tissue based (6 markers). No fecal marker met inclusion criteria (Figure 1). A quality assessment of studies demonstrating significant associations with stricturing CD was performed using the Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies-2 (QUADAS-2)12 (Supplemental Table 1). Overall quality of the studies included was good. Only 5/44 (11%) of studies had a risk of bias or applicability concern in any category rated as ‘High,’ and no study had ‘High’ risk or concern in more than one category. For the sake of readability statistical figures are not being reported in the manuscript body except for key data, but are provided in detail in Table 1 and the Supplemental Results section. In the literature the terms ‘stenosis’, ‘stricture’, ‘fibrostenosis’ and others are used interchangeably without a clear consensus in the field and for the purpose of this review the term ‘stricture’ was chosen. Biomarkers were further considered for their potential to be used in the prediction of stricture formation in patients without stricturing disease, early detection of stricture, and prognostication.

Serum Markers

Twenty-four studies met inclusion criteria and evaluated at least one serum biomarker. Of those, eighteen studies (3988 CD patients) demonstrated a significant association of a serum marker with stricturing disease. Of these, fourteen studies (3343 CD patients) were cross sectional or retrospective. Two studies (179 CD patients) were prospective, and two studies (466 CD patients) were cross sectional with a prospective component (Table 1). A total of 20 individual serum biomarkers were identified.13–30

Anti-microbial antibodies

A critical component in the pathogenesis of inflammatory bowel disease (IBD) is a dysregulated intestinal immune response to intestinal luminal antigens.31 This occurrence, together with a genetic predisposition, results in the formation of anti-microbial antibodies that can be detected in the serum of patients with IBD, in particular CD.32 Multiple studies have evaluated the presence and concentrations of anti-microbial antibodies in the prediction and diagnosis of stricturing disease. Out of eleven eligible studies, eight studies with a total of 1823 CD patients demonstrated a significant association between anti-microbial antibodies and stricturing disease. Of these, six studies (1351 CD patients) were cross sectional, and two studies (472 CD patients) were cross sectional with a prospective component.

Association with stricturing disease (at any time throughout the disease course):

Eleven studies evaluated the association between ASCA and disease behavior or phenotype, with eight articles concluding that elevated ASCA antibody concentrations are associated with stricturing CD and all of which were cross-sectional and used endoscopy, radiographic studies, or histology as reference standard.13–20 These included a total of 3,040 patients, 1,823 (60%) of whom had CD and a minimum of 596 (33% of CD patients) had strictures. Two studies did not report the number of patients that had stricturing CD. Zymogen granule glycoprotein 2 (GP2) is thought to be an important immunomodulatory molecule and anti-GP2 antibodies are associated with CD.33 Two studies met inclusion criteria for evaluation of anti-GP2, both of which utilized endoscopy, radiographic studies, or histology as reference standard and demonstrated an association with stricturing CD, but only one performed multivariate analysis.13, 15 In a cross sectional study with a prospective subgroup in 689 patients, 303 (44%) of whom had CD and 104 (34% of CD patients) had stricturing disease, an association for anti-GP2 IgGα and IgGβ with strictures was noted.13 Anti-GP2 IgA isoforms were not linked with this phenotype. In a prospective subgroup of 70 patients, no difference in prevalence or concentration of anti-GP2 antibodies was noted between patients who progressed to a more complicated disease course relative to those who did not progress. Additional anti-microbial antibodies that demonstrated an association with CD strictures include the anti-flagellins A4-Fla2 (OR 3.41, p=0.027), anti Fla-X (OR 2.95, p=0.041), anti-CBir1 (multiple studies); anti-CD-associated bacterial sequence (I2) (OR not given), anti-Escherichia coli outer membrane porin C (OmpC)(not associated independently on multivariate analysis), as well as the non-antimicrobial (ANCA/pANCA).14, 16, 18–20 In two studies, perinuclear antineutrophil cytoplasmic antibody (pANCA) level was negatively associated with stricture,14, 16 and in a third ANCA and pANCA failed to demonstrate an association.18 All studies also utilized endoscopy, radiographic studies, or histology as a reference standard.

Prediction of future development of strictures:

One study had a prospective sub-group that reported faster progression to fistula, stenosis, or IBD-related surgery in patients with ASCA antibody positivity or higher levels, but did not report progression to stricture independently.13 Another study found ASCA positivity to be associated with stricture at diagnosis and future disease progression to complicated CD.17 However, the prospective component did not look at progression to stricturing disease as an independent outcome.17

The risk of bias and applicability concerns for all discussed sections in this article can be found in Supplemental Table 1. Additional details are included in Table 1 and Supplemental Results. Mechanistically, the presence of anti-microbial antibodies in serum lends itself to use as an early diagnostic tool or prognosticator of strictures as they may reflect the immune response driving stricture formation or stricture progression rather than the fibrotic burden itself. Prospective studies evaluating anti-microbial antibodies and strictures specifically are lacking, and the current data serves to associate several anti-microbial antibodies with the presence of stricture at any time in the disease course but does not demonstrate an ability to predict or prognosticate stricturing disease selectively.

Serum proteins

Serum peptides were evaluated as biomarkers in ten studies, nine of which (2002 CD patients) demonstrated a significant association with stricturing disease.21–26, 28–30 Of these, seven studies (1823 CD patients) were cross sectional or retrospective and two studies (179 CD patients) were prospective (Table 1).

Association with stricturing disease (at any time throughout the disease course):

Several cross-sectional studies evaluated serum proteins using endoscopy, radiology, or histology as a reference standard finding links with mannan-binding lectin (MBL) OR: 2.328 (95% CI, 1.294-3.875) (p=0.016),28 elafin (AUC = 0.657),29 and markers of collagen formation and MMP-mediated degradation (only able to distinguish stricturing from penetrating, highest AUC was for Pro-C3/C3M [AUC: 0.760]).26 Beyond candidate approaches, the emergence of proteomic technology allows for unbiased analysis. Glycoproteomics have identified potential biomarkers of strictures in several organs, including in the intestine.23, 34, 35

Diagnosis of strictures:

One cross sectional study that evaluated 40 CD patients, of which 20 (50% of CD patients) had stricturing disease utilized proteomics and found cartilage oligomeric matrix protein (COMP) and hepatocyte growth factor activator (HGFA) elevated in patients with strictures compared to patients with an inflammatory phenotype (AUC 0.805 and 7.738 respectively).23 This marker profile my well play a future role after a thorough validation program, given its pathophysiologic relevance in fibrogenesis and hence a possible direct link to the fibrotic burden of disease.

Prediction of future development of strictures:

A prospective cohort study with 6-18 months follow-up evaluated the antimicrobial peptide LL-37 and C-reactive protein in 245 patients, 95 (39%) of whom had CD and 29 (31% of CD patients) had stricturing disease.25 The presence of a stricture was determined based upon imaging or colonoscopy. Blood samples were collected at baseline and clinical characteristics were evaluated at 6-18 months after the initial blood draw. Low LL-37 levels (< 40 ng/mL) had an increased relative risk for stricture, but a high CRP (≥0.5 mg/mL) was not associated with stricture. The prospective nature of this study suggests lower LL-37 levels may have a use in the prediction of stricture RR: 1.7752 (95% CI, 1.0-3.1) (p=0.0485). At this time the pathophysiological relevance of this finding is unclear.

Serum miRNA concentrations

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are non-coding RNAs involved in the post-transcriptional regulation of select biological processes, including intestinal inflammation and strictures.36

Association with stricturing disease (at any time throughout the disease course):

Two studies met inclusion criteria evaluating miRNAs, one found a significant association with stricturing disease.27 This cross sectional study with a validation cohort and a prospective cohort evaluated 168 patients, 163 (97%) of whom had CD, with 57 (35% of CD patients) having strictures. Lower miR-19-3p (a and b) were associated with (AUC=0.67) but not predictive of strictures.27 The lack of predictive ability may be driven by the small sample size as a statistical trend was observed. miRNAs are a promising future tool, but are largely promiscuous in their biologic activity, so their specific association of prediction of stricturing disease will need to be determined.

Genetic Variants/SNPs

CD has long been recognized as having a genetic predisposition based upon familial association and twin studies.37, 38 Twenty-six studies met inclusion criteria and evaluated genetic variants and their association with stricturing CD. Of these, 22 studies (7,308 CD patients) found an association of a genetic variant with stricturing disease. These included two cross sectional studies (395 CD patients), two retrospective cohort studies (1,158 CD patients), 15 case-control studies (3,924 CD patients), and three prospective cohort studies (1,831 patients; Table 1). Nine potential genetic markers were identified.30, 39–59

NOD2/CARD

Nucleotide binding and oligomerization domain (NOD), which was later named caspase-activation recruitment domain (CARD), was the first gene found to be associated with CD susceptibility.60, 61

Association with or prediction of stricturing disease:

Eighteen studies met inclusion criteria and evaluated NOD2/CARD mutations. Of these, fifteen studies with a total number of 4,496 CD patients found an association of a NOD2/CARD variant or mutation with stricturing disease. These included 2 prospective cohort studies (925 patients), 2 retrospective cohort studies (1,158 CD patients), 10 case-control studies (2,138 CD patients), and 1 cross sectional study (275 CD patients; Table 1). Of these, seven studies found an association using multivariate analysis. The highest OR was 15.30 (95% CI, 5.86–39.91).49 Six of these studies had overall low risk of bias and applicability concerns (Supplemental Table 1). Additional details are included in Table 1 and Supplemental Results.

Other Genetic Markers

Eleven studies that met inclusion criteria evaluated genetic variants other than NOD2/CARD.

Association with or prediction of stricturing disease:

Of these, eight studies (3,051 CD patients) found an association of a gene variant or mutation with stricturing disease. These included one cross sectional study with 120 CD patients, six case-control studies (2,070 CD patients), and two prospective cohort studies (1,661 patients; Table 1). Variants that demonstrated an association using multivariate analysis included IL12B polymorphism, AK097548 rs1363670 GG, (OR: 5.48; 95% CI, 1.60-18.83),45 tumor necrosis factor ligand superfamily member (TNFSF) 15 homozygosity for rs6478108 (HR 1.71; 95% CI 1.178 - 2.471), 59 CX3CR1 variant (V249I) (OR 1.808; 95% CI, 1.01–3.23),51 activated protein C resistance (aPCR, factor V Leiden) (no HR or OR reported),56 interleukin (IL) 10 variant rs3024505 (OR: 1.65; 95% CI 1.11 - 2.45),58 and rs11861007 SNP of WW domain containing oxidoreductase (WWOX) (meta-analysis of a discovery and replication cohort had (OR: 3.2, p=6.09x10−11).57 These studies all had overall low risk of bias and applicability concerns (Supplemental Table 1). Additional details are included in Table 1 and Supplemental Results.

Mechanistically, the presence of a genetic variant that is associated with stricture would make a good candidate to predict future formation of stricture or progression of already existing strictures. The inherent stability of genetic variants would not lend itself for stricture diagnosis, detection or link with the current fibrotic burden. Genetic variation in cytokines such as IL12B,45 tumor necrosis factor ligand superfamily member (TNFSF) 15,59 and interleukin (IL) 1058 are notably promising markers as they have potential mechanistic link to the underlying disease process. Although mutations in NOD2/CARD are established to be associated with CD, NOD2/CARD mutations failed to reliably show a specific relation to strictures. A significant limitation is the fact that genetic variants cannot account for the significant impact of the environment on disease courses, such as the microbiome or nutrition and hence will likely be a combination partner together with other predictive biomarkers.

DISCUSSION

Our systematic review found a large number of studies investigating serum markers for CD strictures. While many studies demonstrate ASCA positivity as being associated with stricturing CD, ASCA antibodies were also associated with CD in general, and other disease subtypes such as penetrating disease or ileal location.62–64 The latter observation calls into question the specificity of ASCA serology for the stricturing phenotype. The potential for use of quantitative ASCA titers as a biomarker for intestinal stricture was also explored, but few studies reported specific levels of ASCA, and none did so on a per patient basis. Additionally there are no commonly accepted cut-offs for titers and in fact baseline values across populations may vary.65 Our assessment based on this analysis questions the clinical value of anti-microbial antibodies as biomarkers of strictures, owing primarily to a lack of specificity for any select phenotypes and fluctuating concentrations over time.66, 67 While less explored in general, a comparable situation is evident for anti-GP2 antibodies. In addition to anti-microbial antibodies, our review identified CTLA4,21 AHSG,22 MBL,28 COMP,23 HGFA,23 neuropeptide substance P,24 LL-37,25 and markers of collagen formation and turnover26 as potential protein markers associated with stricturing CD. Given the complexity of their function, and the fact that circulating proteins may not reflect processes within the affected tissue, a major limitation to their implementation in clinical care will be identifying proteins and cutoff values that are specific and sensitive for intestinal stricture and not for other forms of inflammation or immune disease. The fluctuation of the levels of these markers over time has not been explored to date.

Multiple genetic biomarkers are linked with stricturing CD, most prominently NOD2/CARD15. Among individual NOD2/CARD15 variants, 1007fs was most commonly associated with stricturing disease.30, 39, 41–52 In contrast, for R702W and G908R variants no association was found.51 In a separate study that did not evaluate stricture independently but did assess progression to severe phenotype (stricturing or penetrating), variant NOD2/CARD15 alleles were protective against rapid progression to a more severe phenotype. These protective effects appeared to be specifically related to the R702W allele (OR 0.38; CI 0.007 to 0.91).68 These disparate results highlight that currently use of NOD2/CARD mutations as a biomarker for strictures needs additional investigation.

We identified six potential histologic or tissue-based biomarkers of intestinal stricture.69–73 The use of histology in clinical practice for this purpose suffers from two important obstacles. Stricture is a transmural process, limiting the value of endoscopic biopsies for diagnosing intestinal stricture. The second major limitation is a lack of validated histopathologic scoring systems.9 While surgical specimens can be utilized for reliably identifying intestinal stricture, by this time the intestine has already been resected, and the identification of stricture in the removed segment is of less clinical value than if it had been identified earlier. Endoscopically, strictures appear as luminal narrowing, however, no gross mucosal changes are known that reliably identify underlying fibrosis, and in early strictures the endoscopic findings are often normal. In this regard, if an aspect of endoscopic biopsies could identify the presence of underlying fibrosis could prove to be an important biomarker. Along the same lines, no fecal marker, arguably a reflection of the state of the superficial mucosa, could be identified to be linked with stricture.

Our understanding of the underlying mechanisms of intestinal fibrosis and thus biological links between biomarkers and stricture remains limited. Some markers are plausibly generated via the formation and degradation of fibrotic lesion, e.g. markers of collagen formation and turnover.26 Variants in in NOD2 may cause an imbalance in immune responses to bacteria, resulting in perturbation of homeostasis, inflammation, and ultimately fibrosis,1 with development of certain anti-microbial antibodies being a reflection of this dysregulation. Much more work is needed to understand the mechanisms of intestinal fibrosis and the links between fibrosis and potential markers of intestinal stricture.

This review demonstrates a paucity of prospective studies, particularly prospective longitudinal inception cohorts to potentially identify predictive markers of future strictures. Similar studies in IBD have recently identified potential biomarkers of future CD in undiagnosed patients.74 Of note, at least three large prospective studies in pediatric patients, and hence not part of our analysis in adult patients, are worth mentioning as they demonstrate the ability to predict strictures and/or penetrating disease using biomarkers of antimicrobial antibodies such as ASCA and anti-CBir1,75, 76 genetic markers such as NOD2 variants,75 and a novel extracellular matrix gene signature. 77 The ability to predict development of strictures could have a powerful impact on clinical decision making, allowing for early therapeutic intervention in an effort to thwart future intestinal obstruction and surgery.

A critical challenge in the development of anti-fibrotic therapies is the lack of a clear and standardized definition of strictures for use in clinical trials. This void is also strikingly evident throughout this review and represents a limitation. In most papers, only cursory definitions are provided that include generic statements indicating the use of a modality, rather than specific and reliable criteria within or across modalities. These papers frequently categorize patients by Montreal or Vienna classification into B1: non-stricturing/non-penetrating, B2: stricturing or B3: penetrating. This creates problems, as demonstrated in57, where the disease group (stricture) was associated with the SNP rs11861007 of WWOX (p=6.09x10−11), but no association with Montreal Class B2 was noted (p=0.25). It needs to be mentioned that many studies included in this review were published before the wide-spread use of cross-sectional imaging, the most accurate method to identify strictures,8 which in turn can impact the reported accuracy of reported biomarkers. Thus, lack of detailed, centralized and standardized independent assessment for stricturing disease is a limitation of our findings, and the validity of any biomarker must be evaluated in that context. To complicate matters, reliable and validated full thickness histologic scoring systems for CD strictures are lacking.9 While frequently employed for diagnosis of stricture, endoscopy showed poor reliability when tested as a component of the simple endoscopic score for CD (SES-CD).78 To advance the field of intestinal stricture further, future work should focus on using clearly defined and widely accepted stricture definitions. A final important challenge is the discovery of non-specific associations. The link between a biomarker and intestinal stricture may not be exclusive, and also associated with other aspects of CD such as penetrating disease, ileal location, or simply CD compared to UC or healthy controls. Markers that are specific to intestinal strictures are needed.

Despite the identification of a number of potential biomarkers, none are validated, reliable, and clinically applicable at this time. The pursuit of a biomarker for stricturing CD should be a research priority. The clinical scenarios and respective study designs on how to test them are depicted in Figure 2. To overcome this challenge, the Stenosis Therapy and Anti-fibrotic Research (STAR) consortium is currently developing definitions and outcome measures for stricture that will serve as the basis for future robust biomarker development.8–10 This includes imaging and histopathology indices. After establishing a strong association of a marker with strictures, its responsiveness and operating characteristics need to be further elucidated in prospective randomized trials testing drugs with an anti-fibrotic mechanism.

The future of biomarkers for stricturing disease has just begun. All biomarkers discussed in this review are yet to be validated based on a rigorous gold standard. On the basis of a robust reference standard then novel approaches can be employed.79–81 Promising future directions include the combination of multiomics approaches with clinical diagnostics, such as imaging. Further, with the advent of next generation sequencing technologies we have gained the ability to decode genetic and phenotypic signatures of a tissue down to single cellular resolution.82 Lastly, a recent study for the first time utilized machine learning to identify protein markers that predict stricturing or penetrating CD in pediatric patients.81 The application of artificial intelligence for prediction and diagnosis of stricturing CD will profoundly impact the field. Due to the high morbidity and economic impact of stricturing CD, a biomarker of intestinal stricture would have the potential to dampen the significant negative impact for patients, their caregivers, and the healthcare system at large.

WHAT YOU NEED TO KNOW

BACKGROUND:

Biomarkers of intestinal strictures could support the clinical management of Crohn’s disease patients and aid in the stratification and monitoring of patients during clinical trials of future antifibrotic drug candidates.

FINDINGS:

Well-defined studies on biomarkers of intestinal stricture are lacking. Despite some promising candidates, currently no biomarkers of intestinal strictures exist that are validated, reliable, and clinically applicable.

IMPLICATIONS FOR PATIENT CARE:

Several obstacles need to be overcome in development of a biomarker for intestinal strictures. A biomarker of intestinal strictures would have the potential to dampen the negative impact for patients, their caregivers, and the healthcare system at large.
